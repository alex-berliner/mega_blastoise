# Mega Blastoise single-screen stripboard - build guide

Generated by `layout.py`. Do not edit by hand; edit the geometry there and
re-run it. The KiCad schematic in this directory is the electrical
authority, this file is the physical one, and both come from the same map.

## 0. What this board is

ONE 24x56 stripboard (61 x 142 mm, strips running the
long way) carrying a Raspberry Pi Pico 2, one 3.5" ST7796 320x480 SPI
display standing portrait in the middle, and a BAND at each end holding
that player's two compact button crosses side by side - eight buttons per
player, d-pad cross on the player's left, action cross on their right.

The compact cross interlocks its four switches: N/S centres are 10.16 mm
apart, W/E 20.3 mm, everything else 11.4 mm. **Button caps must be 9 mm**
- the old 11.3 mm caps collide at this pitch. Cap edge clearance at the
tightest pairs is 1.2 mm.

**Strips run top to bottom** (Player 2's edge to Player 1's edge), one
strip per column. Three straight cut lines organise the board: fences on
rows 12 and 44 isolate the two button bands, and
row 30 splits every column under the Pico so the middle of
each strip belongs to exactly one Pico pin. Buttons then need one wire
each: band strip to GPIO strip.

There is no button matrix and there are no diodes. All 16 switches go
straight to their own GPIO with the internal pull-up enabled, so ghosting
cannot happen. The LiPo cell mounts on the BACK (copper side) on a foam
pad; the TP4056 mounts on the FRONT under the display deck with its
USB-C overhanging the right edge, so cell and charger sit on opposite
faces and the cell's leads come round the edge to BT1.

## 2. Coordinates

Holes are named the usual stripboard way: **letter then number**, the
letter being the column (A..X, left to right) and the
number the row (1..56, from Player 2's edge to Player 1's). So E12
is column 5, row 12. Hole A1 sits
1.27 mm in from the left edge and 1.27 mm down from
the top. All views are of the component side.

## 3. Cut the tracks first

43 cuts. Do every one before any part goes down; most sit under
the Pico and the display and cannot be reached later. A cut destroys the
hole it names. Verify each with a meter before moving on.

**Row 30, columns C-V** is the
structural line: it splits every one of the Pico's columns into a
Player 2 half above and a Player 1 half below. Cut it first.

The rest are per-column fences. They do NOT line up into rows - each one
is placed as close to the Pico as its own pads allow, because a fence
decides how far a net can reach and therefore how long its wire has to
be. Work down this list, or print `cut_template.svg` at 100% scale, tape
it to the copper side and cut every cross on it.

| Column | Cut at | Holes |
|---|---|---|
| A | row 44 | A44 |
| D | row 12 | D12 |
| H | rows 5, 12, 52 | H5 H12 H52 |
| I | rows 12, 45 | I12 I45 |
| J | row 35 | J35 |
| L | row 47 | L47 |
| M | rows 12, 47 | M12 M47 |
| O | row 35 | O35 |
| P | row 48 | P48 |

## 6. Jumper wires

15 wires. Insulated, on the component side, soldered **only** at
the holes listed. The two buses are one wire each with several joints.
Endpoints are the closest free hole pair on the two strips each wire
joins, and each fence cut is slid as close to the Pico as its own pads
allow, since where a cut sits decides how far a net can reach. Together
that is 368 mm of wire, longest single run 48 mm.

Wires may pass under a switch body - thread them between the legs before
the switch goes in and it still seats flat. Three wires also run beside a
ground bus; that is allowed, it just means dressing them apart by eye.
Wires that share a row simply lie over each other - only the endpoints
are electrical.

Wire colours follow a standard 6-colour kit and match the preview:
blue = P1 buttons, green = P2 buttons, yellow = display signals,
red = power (3V3, VSYS), white = ground, orange = battery sense.

## 7. Build order

1. Make all cuts in section 3. Meter every one.
2. Solder the jumper wires in section 6. They run under the Pico and the
   display, so they must go down first. No solder joint sits under the
   Pico's body - every endpoint stays reachable for rework.
3. Solder the Pico. Headers on the Pico first, then drop it in with the
   USB pointing left, check it straddles the row 30 cuts,
   then solder.
4. Solder the 16 switches, all four legs each.
5. Solder D1, R1, R2, C1, the J1 socket and the 2-pin headers.
6. Wire the charger module, cell and slide switch to their headers.
7. Foam-pad the cell to the back of the board under the display area.
8. Plug the display into the J1 socket at columns 9-16, row 46.
9. With the module seated, shim the four adhesive standoffs to the gap
   under its corners, then peel and stick them to the board. Doing this
   last lets them find their own height instead of fighting the socket.

## 8. Power

```
USB-C -> TP4056 (protected) -> OUT+ -> slide switch -> 1N5817 -> VSYS
                            -> OUT- -> GND
```

The charger's USB-C and the Pico's micro-USB may be plugged in at the same
time. VBUS reaches VSYS through the Pico's own internal Schottky and the
cell reaches it through D1, so the higher rail simply wins - about 4.7 V
from USB against about 3.9 V from a full cell - and neither source can push
current back into the other. Charge current is set by the module's
programming resistor; 1 A (1.2 kohm) suits a 2000 mAh cell.

Battery sense: cell through R1/R2 (100k/100k) into GP28 (ADC2), filtered by
C1. The divider hangs off the switched side, so it draws nothing when the
console is off. Reading is half the cell voltage: 2.1 V at 4.2 V full,
1.5 V at 3.0 V empty.

### The charger

The TP4056 is 26 x 17 mm and sits on the
FRONT with its top-left corner near hole O13,
right-aligned so the USB-C hangs 1 mm past the board edge. It is about
4.8 mm tall including the connector, well under the 8.5 mm
display deck it lives beneath. Stick it down on foam tape once its four
leads are soldered.

It has no pads on the board of its own - it reaches the circuit entirely
through four flying leads:

| Module pad | Goes to | Hole |
|---|---|---|
| B+ | J2.1 | W10 |
| B- | J2.2 | X10 |
| OUT+ | J3.1 | W21 |
| OUT- | J3.2 | X21 |

The cell's own leads land on BT1 at W8 and X8, which share those
strips with J2 - that is how cell and charger meet.

Cell on the back, charger on the front. The cell's leads come round the
board edge to BT1; keep them clear of the P1 button caps.

## 9. Display and access

The module is 55.5 x 98.0 mm with a
48.96 x 73.44 mm active area, portrait in
the middle, split into two 320x240 halves with the far half rotated 180
degrees. It spans the board's full width less 2.7 mm a side, and clears
the nearest button caps by about half a millimetre at each band - check
cap seating before final assembly.

### Holding the display down

The 8-pin socket carries the electrical connection but the module
cantilevers about 94 mm past it, so the far end needs support. Four
**self-adhesive nylon standoffs** (8 mm tall,
~10 mm base) sit under the module's corners at holes:

- C9
- V9
- C47
- V47

Nothing is drilled. The pads bond to the bare component side and the
copper is on the reverse, which is why two of these can sit over live
power strips without consequence. Screws were rejected: the module's own
corner holes are roughly 50 x 92 mm apart and the 0.1 in grid can only
land on multiples of 2.54 mm, so they will not line up.

Deck height is set by the socket, about 8.5 mm. Plug the
module in first, measure the real gap under each corner, then shim each
standoff with washers or foam tape before peeling the backing - stick
them down last, with the module in place, so they self-align.

**Verify the pin order against the module's silkscreen before soldering.**
This layout assumes the common order 1=GND 2=VCC 3=SCL/SCK 4=SDA/MOSI
5=RES 6=DC 7=CS 8=BLK. If your module differs, the fix is to re-target the
eight display jumpers in section 6, not to move the socket.

The Pico's micro-USB faces the left board edge under the display overhang;
the plug (8 mm tall) fits under the module on its 10 mm standoffs, but it
is snug - check with your cable. BOOTSEL ends up under the display too:
unplug the module from its socket to reach it, or flash over SWD/picotool.
The case needs ~10 mm of depth behind the board for the cell.

There is no buzzer: this console is silent by design.
