# Density, gloss and 13.5 mm make a DS Lite

A Nintendo DS Lite (USG-001) bottom half feels like a DS Lite because it is a small, dense, crisply edged slab: Nintendo's **218 g in a 133.0 x 73.9 x 21.5 mm closed box works out to 1.03 g/cm³**, and nearly everything heavy (mainboard, both cartridge slots, battery, stylus, every control) sits in the bottom half ([Nintendo JP spec](https://www.nintendo.co.jp/ds/spec/); [FCC internal photos](https://fccid.io/BKEUSG-001/Internal-Photos/Internal-Photos-1-626991)). That half is **about 13.5 mm thick at its rim, not the 10.75 mm that halving 21.5 suggests**; its touchscreen frame stands 1.8 to 2.3 mm higher still and nests into a concave 7.9 mm lid when closed ([BitBuilt scan](https://bitbuilt.net/forums/posts/65514/)). Its silhouette is one glossy back shell: a flat back that rolls into near-vertical walls through a **4.2 mm radius**, with **4.3 to 4.4 mm plan corners** and a 0.6 to 0.8 mm lip around a matte face plate set just inside it, so the glossy-outside, matte-inside contrast that Nintendo named as a design point falls exactly on the parting line ([N.O.M hands-on](http://web.archive.org/web/20060405020911/http://www.nintendo.co.jp:80/nom/0604/41/index.html); [Good Design Award 2006](https://www.g-mark.org/gallery/winners/9d46dc0b-803d-11ed-862b-0242ac130002)). The controls feel soft, raised and quiet because the D-pad and ABXY are hard caps on carbon-pill silicone domes with **about twice the original DS's stroke**; the D-pad rocks on a peg that passes through its membrane onto bare board, while L and R click crisply on side-push tact switches ([Game Watch](https://game.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/20060306/dsl.htm); [Pi Tin build guide](https://github.com/jackw01/pi-tin/blob/main/hardware_build.md)). Nintendo's own record (spec, manuals, interviews, FCC photos and design patent **US D554,194**, whose plan view turns out to be drawn to scale) is solid, but every sub-millimetre shell number comes from a scan of an aftermarket shell, every cap number from replicas, and nobody has published part masses or a balance point for a genuine unit. A kitchen scale and calipers on the owner's genuine DS Lite can close most of those gaps (see "Measure your own unit"). For our case, the 14.6 mm screen block already sits within about 1 mm of the DS Lite's frame height; the 11.06 mm button decks, the square back edge, the 8 mm corners, the side-wall seam and the mass (DS Lite density means about **160 g**) are where it departs most.

## Three proxies stand in for a genuine shell

Only three kinds of number in this report come from genuine Nintendo hardware: Nintendo's own published figures, iFixit's measurements on genuine units (screw lengths, for example), and gorgyrip's scan of a genuine C/USG-CPU-10 mainboard redrawn in Eagle ([BitBuilt board thread](https://bitbuilt.net/forums/posts/65656/); [C-USG-CPU-10.brd](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/DeaDDFuze/DSLITE_Remastered/main/pcb/original/C-USG-CPU-10.brd)). **No caliper study or 3D scan of a genuine shell has been published.** Shell geometry therefore rests on three proxies that check each other: Wesk's 3D scan of a third-party shell, whose outline matches Nintendo's to within 0.3 % ([BitBuilt post](https://bitbuilt.net/forums/posts/65514/); [scan thread](https://bitbuilt.net/forums/threads/ds-lite-scan-3rd-party.6172/)); the plan view of Nintendo's design patent scaled to 133.0 mm wide, which matches the genuine board to about 0.5 mm ([D554194 PDF](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/46/a7/2c/bfb99f34860929/USD554194S1.pdf)); and replica models built to take genuine caps, chiefly Pi Tin ([github.com/jackw01/pi-tin](https://github.com/jackw01/pi-tin)). ## A 13.5 mm glossy tub with a matte face nested inside

Nintendo publishes only the closed envelope: **133.0 mm wide, 73.9 mm deep, 21.5 mm thick, about 218 g**, against 148.7 x 84.7 x 28.9 mm and 275 g for the original DS ([Nintendo JP spec](https://www.nintendo.co.jp/ds/spec/index.html); [press release](https://www.nintendo.co.jp/corporate/release/2006/060126.html)). It never splits the thickness between the halves, and splitting it evenly is wrong because the halves do not meet on a flat plane. The aftermarket scan puts the bottom half's rim at **13.3 to 13.6 mm** and the top half at **about 7.9 mm**, a sum of 21.4 mm against the official 21.5 ([BitBuilt scan](https://bitbuilt.net/forums/posts/65514/)). The sum works because the halves interlock in relief: the lid's inner face is concave, its rim standing about 2.1 mm proud of its centre, and the bottom half's raised screen frame drops into that recess, so the closed thickness is rim plus rim rather than rim plus rim plus frame. A 2006 reader described the lid the same way, "raised around the edges, making it concave" ([NWR](https://nintendoworldreport.com/news/11047/ds-lite-details)), Nintendo's magazine says the recess doubles as a thumb grip when the console is held like a book ([N.O.M 4-1](http://web.archive.org/web/20060405020911/http://www.nintendo.co.jp:80/nom/0604/41/index.html)), and Shacknews noted the "raised bezel" keeps the screens from touching ([Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/41445/nintendo-ds-lite-enamel-navy)). An independent part agrees: facelesstech's printed replacement for the DS Lite's back shell is 133.0 x 73.95 x 13.5 mm, though its author does not say how it was measured ([Printables 205873](https://www.printables.com/model/205873)); the scanned back shell's own bounding box is 13.78 mm tall ([Wesk download](https://bitbuilt.net/wesk/3D%20Scans/DS%20Lite.rar)).

The bottom half is not one thickness. The button "wings" either side of the screen sit at 13.1 to 13.55 mm, just under the back shell's lip; the frame around the touchscreen rises **1.8 to 2.3 mm** above them, not on a straight ramp but on a **concave cove** (radius about 12 to 14.5 mm) that is steepest, 30 to 33 degrees, at a narrow crest just outside the window and flattens into the wings over about 7 mm, ending where the D-pad opening begins; the strip between the hinge knuckles drops to 11.7 to 12.0 mm under the top half's barrel; and the left hinge knuckle climbs to 21.2 mm, nearly the full closed thickness ([BitBuilt scan](https://bitbuilt.net/forums/posts/65514/)).

In plan the bottom half is a **132.6 to 133.2 mm by 73.6 to 73.9 mm rounded rectangle** with sides straight to within 0.2 mm, front corners of **r 4.3 to 4.4 mm**, and a 92.4 mm hinge section in the middle of the back edge that leaves a 20.4 x 9.4 mm notch at each back corner for L and R ([BitBuilt scan](https://bitbuilt.net/forums/posts/65514/)). In section the back shell is a tub: a flat back, a **4.2 mm radius** blend into a side wall that is nearly vertical but flares about 0.4 mm outward on the way up (about 2 degrees of draft), and a thin 0.6 to 0.8 mm lip at the top. Because the plan radius and the section radius are nearly equal, each front corner reads as a near-uniform 4.2 to 4.4 mm ball. The face plate drops inside that lip onto a ledge about 1.5 mm below its top and finishes 0.1 to 0.3 mm under it with a roughly 0.5 mm edge round, so **the only visible seam on the bottom half runs around the top face, 0.6 to 0.8 mm in from the outer edge, and the side walls are one unbroken surface of the back shell**. The patent's plan view draws a double line about 0.8 mm inside each side edge, consistent with that lip ([D554194 PDF](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/46/a7/2c/bfb99f34860929/USD554194S1.pdf)). The shells close with seven screws from the back plus perimeter snap fits, which iFixit opens by running a spudger "between the lower and upper case" around the unit, noting that "some units are sealed with adhesive" ([iFixit guide 4636](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Lower+Case+Replacement/4636)).

The face is the "rectangle inside a rectangle" that Nintendo's designer wanted a child to be able to draw ([Iwata Asks: DSi](https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/ds/dsi/0/2/)): a centred touchscreen window inside the raised frame, the D-pad alone on the left wing, and on the right the ABXY diamond with START above SELECT in a column directly under the Y button ([BitBuilt scan](https://bitbuilt.net/forums/posts/65514/); [DS Lite manual](https://web.archive.org/web/2015id_/http://www.nintendo.com/consumer/downloads/DSLite_english.pdf)). **Three independent records of that layout agree to about 0.5 mm**: the aftermarket scan, the patent's plan view scaled to 133.0 mm, and the genuine board's element positions plus the board's inset from the shell walls (4.37 mm each side, about 2.0 mm at the front and 2.2 mm at the hinge) ([D554194 PDF](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/46/a7/2c/bfb99f34860929/USD554194S1.pdf); [C-USG-CPU-10.brd](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/DeaDDFuze/DSLITE_Remastered/main/pcb/original/C-USG-CPU-10.brd)). The two exceptions are the D-pad's distance from the front edge (patent and board differ by about 1 mm) and the ABXY pitch, which the reconciliation section takes up. ## Clear over colour outside, matte inside, split at the parting line

Nintendo's mechanical designer Sakiyama said the DS Lite's "distinctive texture" comes from **two-colour (two-shot) moulding**: Crystal White and Ice Blue use a transparent part and Enamel Navy a pale blue translucent part, and because of the two-shot process the colour-check prototypes, normally made in-house, went to an outside specialist ([N.O.M 4-2](http://web.archive.org/web/20060405020918/http://www.nintendo.co.jp:80/nom/0604/42/index.html)). Nintendo's magazine described the result as a body surface with "a gloss as if a glass cover had been placed over it", while the area around the D-pad and ABXY is deliberately matte, and "the contrast of the two textures, inside versus outside, creates a distinctive premium feel" ([N.O.M 4-1](http://www.nintendo.co.jp/nom/0604/41/index.html)); the Good Design Award entry lists that texture contrast as one of its three design points ([Good Design Award 2006](https://www.g-mark.org/gallery/winners/9d46dc0b-803d-11ed-862b-0242ac130002)). A pre-launch mockup teardown found "a layer of transparent resin outside the white resin" that shows clearly when lit from behind ([PC Watch](https://pc.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/2006/0216/nintendo.htm)), and owners still find dirt trapped in a hairline gap between the clear skin and the coloured body on white and pink units ([GBAtemp 64176](https://gbatemp.net/threads/gunk-stunk-inside-clear-plastic-white-nds-lite.64176/); [GBAtemp 173506](https://gbatemp.net/threads/cleaning-nintendo-ds-lite.173506/); [Yahoo! Chiebukuro](https://detail.chiebukuro.yahoo.co.jp/qa/question_detail/q1333204243)). The process was hard: Ice Blue and Enamel Navy slipped nine days for "unexpected trouble in the manufacturing process" ([Nintendo release 2006-02-24](https://www.nintendo.co.jp/corporate/release/2006/060224.html)), and a later Nintendo designer said two-tone colourways never worked on the Lite because "the hinges are quite complicated" ([Iwata Asks: DSi XL](https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/ds/xl/0/3/)).

On Crystal White the glossy clear parts are the outer face of the lid and the outer face of the bottom half's back ([Game Watch 2006-03-06](https://game.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/20060306/dsl.htm)); the FCC photos show specular highlights on the lid, back, battery cover and side band, with the face around the touchscreen reading satin ([FCC external photos](https://fccid.io/BKEUSG-001/External-Photos/External-Photos-626995)); and GAME Watch saw the side-printed "POWER" stencil appear to float under the crystal layer ([Game Watch 2006-02-15](https://game.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/20060215/nin3.htm)). Put together with the geometry, **the gloss part is the whole back shell (back, sides and battery cover) and the matte part is the whole face plate, so the texture boundary is the 0.6 to 0.8 mm lip seam on the top face**. Reviewers noticed the side effects: the matte inside is "off-tone, so the colors don't match", and the fold edges "appear to be yet another, lighter color" ([NWR review](https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/11365)), which fits light piping through the clear skin's edge. Gloss level was never specified; under the ISO 2813 convention "high gloss" means above about 70 units at 60 degrees, which the "glass cover" finish almost certainly exceeds (Estimate; [ISO 2813 abstract](https://webstore.ansi.org/standards/iso/iso28131994)).

Nintendo never stated the housing material. Its Japanese manual lists the Slot-2 dust cover as "ABS, PC/ABS", the stylus as an ABS body with a PE tip, and the strap as ABS and nylon ([JP manual](https://www.nintendo.co.jp/ds/support/pdf/dsLitemanual.pdf)); a reviewer said the dust cover is "made of the same plastic as the outside" ([NWR review](https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/11365)); and one iFixit photo of a black unit's battery bay shows the moulded code **">ABS,PC+ABS<"** next to "3.7V USG-003" ([iFixit guide 4636](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Lower+Case+Replacement/4636)). The best reading is a two-shot ABS and PC/ABS shell, one material per shot, though no source says which shot is which. The manual also warns that solvents can peel the body's 塗装 (paint or coating) ([JP manual](https://www.nintendo.co.jp/ds/support/pdf/dsLitemanual.pdf)), and some special editions were painted, such as the metallic black Dialga/Palkia edition ([ja.wikipedia](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%83%8B%E3%83%B3%E3%83%86%E3%83%B3%E3%83%89%E3%83%BCDS_Lite)). Claims of "polycarbonate" or "1.2 mm ABS" shells come from unsourced shopping guides ([Alibaba guide](https://electronics.alibaba.com/buyingguides/nintendo-ds-lite-shell-replacement-guide); [Alibaba Q&A](https://electronics.alibaba.com/question/ds-lite-shell-replacement-guide-fit,-quality-diy-tips)) and from an article that confuses the Lite with the NTR-001 ([Decked Out](https://deckedoutmagazine.com/2026/03/02/the-files-012-nintendo-ds-lite/)). Genuine wall thickness has never been published; the aftermarket scan's 1.27 to 1.5 mm walls sit at the thin end of the 1.14 to 3.56 mm range Protolabs gives for moulded ABS ([Protolabs](https://www.protolabs.com/resources/design-tips/solving-wall-thickness-issues-in-molded-parts/)), and Nintendo described the lid as looking as thick as the DS's but "quite thin seen from inside" ([N.O.M 4-2](http://www.nintendo.co.jp/nom/0604/42/index.html)).

The finish has known wear patterns. Reviewers called the gloss "a magnet for fingerprints", worst on dark colours; NWR and Ars found it harder to scratch than feared, while CNET called darker units "just as susceptible to scratches and smudges" as a PSP ([CNET](https://www.retroisle.com/others/nintendods/Articles/dslite_review_cnet.php); [NWR review](https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/11365); [Ars Technica p.2](http://web.archive.org/web/20060616002105/http://arstechnica.com:80/reviews/hardware/dslite.ars/2)); white L and R buttons go "almost yellow" within about a year ([GBAtemp 64176](https://gbatemp.net/threads/gunk-stunk-inside-clear-plastic-white-nds-lite.64176/)); Pocket Gamer recommends polishing only the gloss, "rubbing away the top layer" ([Pocket Gamer](https://www.pocketgamer.com/how-tos/how-to-remove-ds-lite-scratches)); and one owner sanded the gloss off because they preferred "a nice textured feel in my hands like the inner casing" ([bit-tech](https://bit-tech.net/news/modding/Scratched_up_DS_Lite_Mod/1)). Aftermarket shells are sold as single-shot ABS (eXtremeRate's "premium ABS", plus "Soft Touch", clear and chrome variants) and draw consistent complaints of thin walls, fragile hinge pieces, screw posts that crack or let screws push through, and warping in heat ([HHL eXtremeRate shell](https://handheldlegend.com/products/full-shell-replacement-for-nintendo-ds-lite-extremerate); [GBAtemp 590071](https://gbatemp.net/threads/seeking-suggestions-for-ds-lite-shell-parts.590071/)). A single-shot replica cannot reproduce the glass-cover depth, because there is no clear skin over the colour.

## Soft domes, a centre peg and crisp shoulders

The DS Lite's face buttons feel different from the original DS's for one mechanical reason: **its D-pad and ABXY caps press carbon-pill silicone domes straight onto the board, while the DS, DSi and GBA SP put a metal-dome tact switch under the rubber**. A Japanese launch hands-on found "the button stroke is nearly double" between the DS and the DS Lite, explained it by the different shape of the rubber part, and summed up the result as "the DS feels like clicking a button, while the DS Lite clearly comes with a feeling of pushing" ([Game Watch](https://game.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/20060306/dsl.htm)). Nintendo said it tuned the D-pad and ABXY toward "a feel close to the GameCube controller" ([N.O.M 4-2](http://web.archive.org/web/20060405020918/http://www.nintendo.co.jp:80/nom/0604/42/index.html)), and reviewers heard "a deeper and softer feel" on the face buttons, a D-pad that has lost its predecessor's shallow clickiness and sits higher, and shoulder buttons that "retain more of the original clickiness" ([Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/41445/nintendo-ds-lite-enamel-navy); [NWR hands-on](https://nintendoworldreport.com/hands-on-preview/11190); [Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2013/02/hardware_classics_nintendo_ds_lite)). Any replica that puts hard, clicky tact switches under the D-pad and ABXY will feel like an original DS, not a Lite; a board with bare gold pads under genuine-style membranes inherits the Lite's feel automatically.

The D-pad's pivot is now settled: **a peg on the underside of the cap passes through a hole in the centre of the one-piece, four-dome silicone pad and bears on the board**. Pi Tin's build guide tells builders to "fit the D-pad membrane over the peg at the center of the D-pad" ([Pi Tin build guide](https://github.com/jackw01/pi-tin/blob/main/hardware_build.md)), its render shows the peg standing through the membrane ([Pi Tin render](https://github.com/jackw01/pi-tin/blob/main/images/front_panel_dslite_buttons.png)), every aftermarket D-pad pad has a round centre hole about 3 mm across ([HHL membranes](https://handheldlegend.com/products/nintendo-ds-lite-silicone-button-pads)), and Game Watch felt that "there is a protrusion at the centre of the D-pad" ([Game Watch](https://game.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/20060306/dsl.htm)). The membrane is not the pivot, so **the board needs a flat, hard, bare landing spot under the D-pad centre**, with no via, part, silkscreen or mask edge there. The cap's top is a shallow dish that "only curves gently toward the centre" rather than the original DS's round dimple; Pi Tin's replica rises 0.43 mm from the centre to r 8 mm, close to a 74 mm concave sphere ([Game Watch](https://game.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/20060306/dsl.htm); [Pi Tin STLs](https://github.com/jackw01/pi-tin/blob/main/3d/pi_tin_stl.zip)). Rocking about a centre peg, a diagonal press tilts about an axis at 45 degrees, so each of the two loaded domes sits only 0.71 of a cardinal dome's distance from that axis and compresses less for the same tilt (Derived). That is why diagonals are the first input to fail when domes are worn or slightly too tall, which matches an iFixit owner who needed a third membrane to get diagonals back ([iFixit Q&A 930769](https://www.ifixit.com/Answers/View/930769/D-pad+diagonal+inputs+don't+register+correctly)).

The ABXY caps are round with flat tops and a roughly 0.5 mm edge round, each with a keyed flange retained only from below by the face plate; the underside has a recess about 5.2 mm across into which the silicone plunger (about 3.1 to 3.3 mm) fits, so the cap is guided partly by the plunger ([Pi Tin STLs](https://github.com/jackw01/pi-tin/blob/main/3d/pi_tin_stl.zip); [HHL membranes](https://handheldlegend.com/products/nintendo-ds-lite-silicone-button-pads)). The ABXY pad has **a raised, hollow, diamond-shaped boss at its centre**, which on the DS Lite clears the lid sensor and on any other board is simply a bump the lid must clear between the four caps ([HHL membranes](https://handheldlegend.com/products/nintendo-ds-lite-silicone-button-pads); [facelesstech blog](https://facelesstech.wordpress.com/2022/07/23/ds-lite-clicky-buttons/)). START and SELECT are hollow round caps, START above SELECT, on a two-plunger strip whose plungers stand in square wells; reviewers found them "very stiff", "tiny" and so alike that it is "difficult to figure out which button is which without looking" ([NWR hands-on](https://nintendoworldreport.com/hands-on-preview/11190); [Ars Technica p.4](https://web.archive.org/web/2006/http://arstechnica.com/reviews/hardware/dslite.ars/4); [Pi Tin build guide](https://github.com/jackw01/pi-tin/blob/main/hardware_build.md)). Each shoulder button is three loose pieces, a plastic trigger, a metal pin trapped between the two shells and a small spring, pressing a right-angle SMD tact switch at the board's back edge; the click comes from that switch's metal dome, which is also the usual failure ([iFixit guide 1922](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Disassembling+Nintendo+DS+Lite+Bottom+Case/1922); [Retro Revival](https://www.retrorevival.ca/2022/04/06/nintendo-ds-lite-shoulder-button-repair); [HHL bumper switches](https://handheldlegend.com/products/copy-of-replacement-bumper-button-for-game-boy-advance-sp-2x)). The power control is a spring-return SMD slide switch (SW13) on the underside of the board, worked by a separate shell slider and made deliberately firm so it would not switch in a bag; you "must hold the Power Button in the UP position for a moment" to turn the unit off ([repair.wiki](https://repair.wiki/w/Nintendo_DS_Lite_not_turning_on); [N.O.M 4-2](http://web.archive.org/web/20060405020918/http://www.nintendo.co.jp:80/nom/0604/42/index.html); [DS Lite manual](https://web.archive.org/web/2015id_/http://www.nintendo.com/consumer/downloads/DSLite_english.pdf)).

No grams-force or travel figure exists in public for any DS Lite control. The usable numbers are analogues: the GBA SP's Alps tact switches at 1.0 N (about 100 gf) and 0.25 mm travel ([Alps SKRRAAE010](https://tech.alpsalpine.com/e/products/detail/SKRRAAE010/); [HHL GBA SP switches](https://handheldlegend.com/products/replacement-tactile-switches-for-game-boy-advance-sp)), and a keypad maker's design rules of 80 to 150 g actuation for short keys, a 40 to 60 % snap ratio, a stroke "as near 0.8 mm as possible" and 30 to 35 g of return force "to ensure that keys do not stick" ([Diamond HMI guide](https://www.diamondhmi.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Rubber-keypad-design-guide-2025.pdf)). The same guide asks for at least 0.4 mm (one axis) and 0.3 mm (the other) between key and case opening. The genuine-style shell gives an ABXY cap about 0.25 to 0.3 mm a side (a 7.5 to 7.6 mm hole on a 7.0 mm cap, aftermarket scan) and retains it purely by its flange; Pi Tin runs tighter at 0.17 mm a side yet still tells builders the caps "should fit loosely" and to trim flanges if needed ([Pi Tin build guide](https://github.com/jackw01/pi-tin/blob/main/hardware_build.md)). Too tight and the weak silicone return cannot beat friction; too loose and caps rock and register only at some angles, the most common complaint about aftermarket pads and caps, alongside presses that need extra force ([GBAtemp 624369](https://gbatemp.net/threads/replacing-buttons-from-refurbished-ds-lite.624369/); [HHL membranes reviews](https://handheldlegend.com/products/nintendo-ds-lite-silicone-button-pads)). There is no published way to tell a genuine membrane from an aftermarket one. The flange pocket must also be deeper than the flange so the case never preloads the dome at rest, and a reshell whose face sits even a few tenths of a millimetre further from the board leaves caps hanging short of contact (Inference from the stack geometry; one iFixit owner reports buttons misbehaving after a shell swap, in an ambiguous post: [iFixit answer 20944](https://nl.ifixit.com/Answers/History/20944)).

## Battery beside the board, board clamped to the face

Everything a player grips lives in the bottom half. Inside it, from the face back: the matte face plate; a separate resistive touch panel held to the window by a black adhesive frame; the 3.0 inch lower LCD in a metal frame, insulated on its back and resting directly on the board; the 6-layer mainboard (C/USG-CPU-01 or -10 at retail, X3 on the FCC sample), with every button contact printed on its front face; the tall parts on its back (Slot-1 card connector in a steel cage, the long Slot-2 connector along the front, the shielded Wi-Fi module with a foam pad, headphone jack, volume slider, DC jack); and finally the back shell, which carries the battery in its own bay beside the slot area, a screwed-in stylus rail between the slots and the battery, and a small stamped steel plate with foam ([FCC internal photos 1](https://fccid.io/BKEUSG-001/Internal-Photos/Internal-Photos-1-626991); [FCC internal photos 3](https://fccid.io/BKEUSG-001/Internal-Photos/Internal-Photos-3-626993); [iFixit guide 4786](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Touchscreen+Replacement/4786); [iFixit guide 4785](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Lower+LCD+Replacement/4785); [BitBuilt "DS lite pcb"](https://bitbuilt.net/forums/threads/ds-lite-pcb.2777/)). The top half holds only the upper LCD, two custom magnet speakers, the mic (in the hinge centre) and the Wi-Fi antenna, joined to the bottom by one flex and two thin wires through the hinge ([iFixit guide 4812](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Speakers+Replacement/4812); [N.O.M 4-2](http://web.archive.org/web/20060405020918/http://www.nintendo.co.jp:80/nom/0604/42/index.html)). Because the battery sits beside the board rather than behind it, **the bottom half's thickness is set by the board plus the Slot-2 connector and cartridge envelope**, the same "battery beside, not behind" trick any thin design needs (Inference). The board outline is 124.26 x 69.22 mm, leaving 4.37 mm per side and about 2 mm front and back for wall and clearance ([C-USG-CPU-10.brd](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/DeaDDFuze/DSLITE_Remastered/main/pcb/original/C-USG-CPU-10.brd)).

There is no metal chassis. Stiffness comes from the two shells with their ribs and bosses, the stylus rail screwed in as a cross beam, the board screwed to the face plate with the LCD bearing on it, components with metal in them, and on some units adhesive around the seam ([FCC internal photos 1](https://fccid.io/BKEUSG-001/Internal-Photos/Internal-Photos-1-626991); [iFixit guide 86279](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Disassembly/86279)). Nintendo said it "raised strength considerably, including reinforcing the internal structure and balancing the placement of parts", while acknowledging "a conflict with weight and size" ([N.O.M 4-2](http://web.archive.org/web/20060405020918/http://www.nintendo.co.jp:80/nom/0604/42/index.html); [GameSpot summary](http://web.archive.org/web/20070129031839/http://www.gamespot.com:80/news/6147089.html)); its award entry cites drop tests from 1.5 m and key tops tested to 5 million presses ([Good Design Award 2006](https://www.g-mark.org/gallery/winners/9d46dc0b-803d-11ed-862b-0242ac130002)). Reviewers called the result "put together just as tightly as other Nintendo handhelds" with "no longer gaps of any kind when it is folded" ([NWR review](https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/11365); [Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/41445/nintendo-ds-lite-enamel-navy)). The design depends on loose parts (L and R pins and springs, caps, membranes) being trapped by the shells, so shell-to-shell registration and boss heights control preload and rattle.

## 218 g at 1.03 g/cm³, with the battery behind the left thumb

The DS Lite's mass is the most under-measured thing about it. Nintendo gives **about 218 g** including the battery and stylus on its spec page, and its Japanese manual says the same 218 g includes the battery, stylus and the 7.1 g Slot-2 cover ([Nintendo JP spec](https://www.nintendo.co.jp/ds/spec/index.html); [JP manual](https://www.nintendo.co.jp/ds/support/pdf/dsLitemanual.pdf)). The stylus is 1.6 g (Official), but nobody has published the weight of either half, the board, either LCD, the shells, or a genuine USG-003 battery. Estimating from energy (3.7 Wh at 150 to 200 Wh/kg) and volume puts the battery at **20 to 25 g**, matching the lighter vendor listings (20 g, 22 g, 27 g) rather than the heavier ones (37 g, 45 g), which probably include packaging ([Subtel](https://www.subtel.nl/en/Battery-for-Nintendo-DS-Lite-USG-001-USG-003-900mAh-Spare-Battery-Replacement-933066.html); [iFixit battery](https://www.ifixit.com/products/nintendo-ds-lite-replacement-battery)). A component tally puts the bottom half at **about 120 to 150 g (55 to 70 % of the total)**, with large uncertainty (Estimate). That bracket is consistent with the whole unit's density: the bottom half's 133.0 x 73.9 x 13.5 mm rim box is 132.7 cm³, so 120 to 150 g is 0.90 to 1.13 g/cm³, and 1.03 g/cm³ gives 137 g (Derived).

**Density, not lightness, is the DS Lite's mass signature.** Nintendo cut 42 % of the original DS's box volume but only 21 % of its mass ([N.O.M 4-1](http://web.archive.org/web/20060405020911/http://www.nintendo.co.jp:80/nom/0604/41/index.html); [Game Watch](https://game.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/20060306/dsl.htm)), and Ars Technica's first impression was that "the unit feels far more dense and satisfying somehow" ([Ars Technica p.2](http://web.archive.org/web/20060616002105/http://arstechnica.com:80/reviews/hardware/dslite.ars/2)). Per square centimetre of hand contact it weighs almost exactly what the original DS did (2.22 against 2.18 g/cm²); it simply got much thinner (Derived). A printed case with a lot of empty volume will feel lighter than its gram count unless it is similarly packed.

No source has measured the centre of gravity, but Nintendo said its mechanical designers lay parts out "while considering the front/back and left/right centre-of-gravity balance when the user holds it" ([N.O.M 4-2](http://web.archive.org/web/20060405020918/http://www.nintendo.co.jp:80/nom/0604/42/index.html)). The one clear asymmetry is the battery: its bay sits on the left half of the back, **behind the D-pad thumb**, spanning most of the depth, with the DC jack on the same side ([BitBuilt scan](https://bitbuilt.net/forums/posts/65514/); [iFixit guide 4636](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Lower+Case+Replacement/4636)). Front to back, the Slot-1 cage and DC jack along the hinge edge roughly balance the Slot-2 connector, dust cover and headphone jack along the front (Inference). When the console is open, the 70 to 100 g lid stands behind the hinge and pulls the held centre of gravity away from the player; a device that copies only the bottom half will therefore feel more front-heavy than an open DS Lite unless mass moves toward its back edge (Inference). A forum user found the Lite sat comfortably where the original DS felt "top heavy" ([Penny Arcade](https://forums.penny-arcade.com/discussion/10617/ergonomics-ds-vs-ds-lite)).

## Designed to look simple and to rest on the fingertips

The DS Lite's industrial designer was **Yui Ehara**, under design manager Kenichi Sugino and producer Satoru Okada, with mechanical design by Kazuo Yoneyama and Tomoyuki Sakiyama; Ehara is also a named inventor on the US design patent ([Good Design Award 2006](https://www.g-mark.org/gallery/winners/9d46dc0b-803d-11ed-862b-0242ac130002); [N.O.M 4-2](http://web.archive.org/web/20060405020918/http://www.nintendo.co.jp:80/nom/0604/42/index.html); [Google Patents D554194](https://patents.google.com/patent/USD554194S1/en)). Nintendo's own three design points were a layout and colour scheme that beginners would not mistake for something "complicated and difficult"; "balance and shape designed for ease of use in landscape, portrait and one-handed holds"; and texture contrast to create "a sense of luxury as an object" ([Good Design Award 2006](https://www.g-mark.org/gallery/winners/9d46dc0b-803d-11ed-862b-0242ac130002)). Yoneyama added the hard constraint: "you cannot shrink it at the cost of operability", so the screen size and button layout stayed, and custom thin parts (upper LCD, speakers) bought the size ([N.O.M 4-2](http://www.nintendo.co.jp/nom/0604/42/index.html); [Engadget summary](https://www.engadget.com/2006-04-05-engineering-the-ds-lite-2-nintendo-hardware-vets-speak.html)). Colours were chosen so "women and older people" could pick them easily and to give a "LIGHT" image; the stylus grew for older users ([N.O.M 4-2](http://web.archive.org/web/20060405020918/http://www.nintendo.co.jp:80/nom/0604/42/index.html)). Ehara later described the line's identity as "two rectangles, top and bottom, with each half containing another rectangle inside", designed "not to stand out too much" because "the console exists for the software" ([Iwata Asks: DSi](https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/ds/dsi/0/2/)). The Apple comparisons that followed ("If Apple were ever to make a handheld gaming console it would look like this") came from the press, never from Nintendo ([Trusted Reviews](https://mail.retroisle.com/others/nintendods/Articles/dslite_review_june2006.php); [NWR hands-on](https://nintendoworldreport.com/hands-on-preview/11190); [Ars Technica p.2](http://web.archive.org/web/20060616002105/http://arstechnica.com:80/reviews/hardware/dslite.ars/2)).

In landscape play nobody wraps the bottom half in a palm. Reviewers describe "resting the system on your fingertips and reaching to the buttons with your thumbs", found it comfortable after a short adjustment with moderate hands, and reported cramping with large ones, NWR noting that "anyone with moderately sized hands will not be able to tightly grip the DSL for any length of time" ([NWR review](https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/11365); [Ars Technica p.4](http://web.archive.org/web/20060616/http://arstechnica.com:80/reviews/hardware/dslite.ars/4); [Quarter to Three](https://forum.quartertothree.com/t/hand-cramps-and-ds-lite/27151)). The hand contacts are therefore the front edge and the two front corners under the fingers and thumb bases, with the index fingers on the low L and R at the back corners (Inference); no source complains of sharp edges. The nearest peer-reviewed data is for one-handed phones: width mattered most, and 8 mm thick with 2.5 mm edge roundness and about 122 g (106 to 137 g comparable) rated best ([Lee et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1177/0018720818792758)); thumb-reach studies find preferred control positions match the thumbs' primary reach zones ([Otten et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1177/0018720812470689)). At about 13.5 mm, the bottom half is roughly 1.7 times the phone optimum but still far too thin for a palm to close around, which is why it rides on the fingertips; its roughly 109 g per hand sits inside the comfortable phone band.

The design-intent research ranked the cues a replica must get right. Ranked by what a viewer notices first: a flat, symmetric rounded rectangle of the right footprint and thickness; gloss outside and matte on the control face; gloss with depth (colour under a clear skin, labels appearing to float); clean surfaces with minimal graphics and no gaps; white first. Ranked by what a user notices in the hand: controls where the DS put them, with the Lite's soft, raised D-pad and ABXY, two small round START and SELECT under ABXY, low L and R hugging the back corners and a firm side power slider; dense, centred weight; a firm lid snap; smooth front corners ([design-intent sources: N.O.M 4-1](http://web.archive.org/web/20060405020911/http://www.nintendo.co.jp:80/nom/0604/41/index.html), [Iwata Asks: DSi](https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/ds/dsi/0/2/), [CNET](https://www.retroisle.com/others/nintendods/Articles/dslite_review_cnet.php), [Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/41445/nintendo-ds-lite-enamel-navy)). Two warnings from Nintendo's own history: going fully matte reads as a DSi, which Nintendo made matte because "fingerprints tended to show up on the black DS Lite systems" ([Iwata Asks: DSi](https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/ds/dsi/0/2/)), and speaker holes belong on the top half, so a bottom-half replica should not add them (Inference from [Iwata Asks: DSi](https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/ds/dsi/0/2/)).

## Where the notes disagree, and which number to design to

**Bottom-half thickness: design to 13.5 mm at the rim.** The design-intent notes used "about 10.75 mm per half", which is 21.5 divided by 2, an assumption rather than a measurement. The geometry notes measured the aftermarket scan at 13.3 to 13.6 mm for the bottom rim and about 7.9 mm for the top half, which sum to within 0.1 mm of Nintendo's 21.5, and the concave lid explains why an even split is impossible ([BitBuilt scan](https://bitbuilt.net/forums/posts/65514/)). facelesstech's replacement back shell (13.5 mm) agrees. The patent's side view gives 14.7 to 15.4 mm, but its side views do not share the plan scale (scaled the same way, they make the closed unit 22.8 to 23.2 mm, 6 to 8 % too thick), so that figure is consistent with the rim and frame heights without refining them ([D554194 PDF](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/46/a7/2c/bfb99f34860929/USD554194S1.pdf)). The correction also changes the ergonomics argument: the half is about 1.7 times the 8 mm phone optimum, not "only slightly thicker".

**Design patent: US D554,194 is the DS Lite; D556,759 is the Game Boy Micro.** D554,194 S1, "Electronic game machine", was filed 21 Jul 2006, issued 30 Oct 2007, names Yui Ehara and Tomoki Yamamoto, and claims JP priority 2006-001637 of 26 Jan 2006, the day the DS Lite was announced; its 15 views show two screens, the D-pad, the ABXY diamond, START and SELECT below it, and a hinge with an LED slot ([Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/USD554194S1/en); [The Register](https://www.theregister.co.uk/2006/01/26/nintendo_redesigns_ds/)). The geometry researcher did not find it because a keyword search returns D556,759 first; both researchers agree D556,759 (filed 14 Nov 2005, single horizontal screen, flat bar body, inventors Kawanobe and Sakiyama) is the Game Boy Micro and a trap ([Google Patents D556759](https://patents.google.com/patent/USD556759S1/en); [PDF](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/d7/22/31/5bf4ec9207fa93/USD556759.pdf)). Its relatives D540,396 and D532,829 are Micro faceplates, and D524,870 and D532,051 claim 2004 priorities that fit the original DS ([D540396](https://patents.google.com/patent/USD540396); [D532829](https://patents.google.com/patent/USD532829); [D524870](https://patents.google.com/patent/USD524870S1/en); [D532051](https://patents.google.com/patent/USD532051S1/en)).

**ABXY pitch: design to 15.0 mm now; let the owner's measurement set the lid holes.** Four numbers measure four different things. The 15.000 mm from the Ampersand breakout's gerbers is a pad pitch read from solder-mask flashes that sit on a 0.5 mm grid, and facelesstech's clicky board gives 15.011 x 15.001 mm; both are boards that work under genuine membranes, so they show what works, rounded or not ([Ampersand on Tindie](https://www.tindie.com/products/ampersand/ds-lite-membrane-pcb/); [facelesstech repo](https://github.com/facelesstech/ds_lite_clicky_buttons)). The 15.14 mm (Y to A) and 15.22 mm (X to B) are element origins in a hand redraw of a scanned genuine board, the only genuine-part source, but footprint origins need not be contact centres ([C-USG-CPU-10.brd](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/DeaDDFuze/DSLITE_Remastered/main/pcb/original/C-USG-CPU-10.brd)). The patent's 15.3 and 15.2 mm are drawn circle centres scaled by assuming the drawn width is exactly 133.0 mm, which carries about 0.15 mm of scale error. The 15.35 mm is the hole spacing in the scanned aftermarket face plate, whose overall scale is good to 0.3 %, so it is a fact about that moulding rather than a scan error ([BitBuilt scan](https://bitbuilt.net/forums/posts/65514/)). The console's own pitch is therefore somewhere between 15.0 and 15.35 mm, most likely 15.1 to 15.3. The disagreement matters less than it looks: each button sits half the pitch from the cluster centre, so a 0.35 mm pitch error moves each pill or cap only 0.18 mm, well inside a 4.52 mm contact pattern and a 7.5 to 7.6 mm hole around a 7.0 mm cap. Keep 15.0 for the pads, which is proven and already in the footprints; if the owner's genuine caps measure 15.2 or more (checklist item 7), move the lid holes to match, because the holes must sit over the membrane's domes.

**ABXY hole and D-pad opening: keep 7.6 mm and an 18.8 mm cross opening for FDM.** No genuine shell has been measured. The numbers come from an aftermarket shell, the patent drawing and a replica, plus two project files:

**Power and charge LEDs: right end of the hinge.** GAME Watch reported the lamps "moved to the right of the hinge that joins the two screens, so they are easy to check when folded", the manual's callouts 3 and 4 point at the right hinge knuckle, the aftermarket face plate has its two LED slits at the right end of the hinge strip, and a later Nintendo engineer said the DS Lite's lights "were all lined up on the hinge" ([Game Watch](https://game.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/20060306/dsl.htm); [DS Lite manual](https://web.archive.org/web/2015id_/http://www.nintendo.com/consumer/downloads/DSLite_english.pdf); [Iwata Asks: 3DS](https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/3ds/how-nintendo-3ds-made/2/4/)). The assumption that they sit near START and SELECT is wrong for the Lite; on the original DS they were below the lower screen. The green power LED turns red on low battery and blinks for wireless and sleep; the orange charge LED lights while charging ([manual](https://web.archive.org/web/2015id_/http://www.nintendo.com/consumer/downloads/DSLite_english.pdf)).

## Conclusion

Three findings change how to approach a DS Lite-like case. First, the DS Lite's look is not a coating applied to a shape; it is a consequence of how the two bottom-half parts nest. The glossy back shell owns the whole silhouette and the matte face plate sits inside its lip, so copying that assembly (a tub with a thin lip, a face nested inside) produces the seam position, the texture split and the unbroken sides at once, while any scheme that puts the seam on the side wall cannot. Second, the feel reduces to a few numbers and one mechanism: about 13.5 mm of body with 4 mm edges, about 1.03 g/cm³, and soft silicone domes under hard caps. A printed device using genuine-style membranes already has the mechanism, and it can buy the density with ballast. Third, the evidence gap is narrow and cheap to close. The community has plenty of CAD; what it lacks is a dozen caliper and scale readings from a genuine shell, and the owner has one.

## Measure your own unit

These need only a kitchen scale, calipers, a round pencil, a coin and a phone camera. Nothing requires opening the shell; item 9 is optional and needs only the one captive battery-cover screw. Ranked by how much each settles. For every thickness, slide the outside jaws about 8 mm in from the edge so the lower jaw sits on the flat back rather than the rounded edge, close them gently, keep the beam square to the back, and touch caps without pressing them (they are sprung). Keep clear of screw holes and the shallow label recess in the middle of the back. Take each reading three times.

1. **Whole-unit weight.**
   How: zero the scale, lay the closed unit flat in the middle, read to 1 g. Weigh (a) as found, with battery, stylus and Slot-2 cover if fitted; (b) with the stylus out; (c) with the Slot-2 cover out, if you have one.
   Dossier now: about 218 g (Nintendo spec: with battery and stylus; JP manual: with battery, stylus and Slot-2 cover). Stylus 1.6 g, cover 7.1 g (JP manual).
   Settles: the reference mass behind the 1.03 g/cm³ density target, and which definition 218 g follows.
2. **Bottom-half thickness at the rim and on the wings.**
   How: open the lid. (a) Rim on the front edge, 15 mm in from the right corner: back face to the top edge of the shell wall. (b) Rim on the right end, 15 mm back from the front corner (clear of the stylus notch). (c) Wing: back face to the face plate surface between the B button and the right end.
   Dossier now: rim 13.3 to 13.6 mm, wings 13.1 to 13.55 mm (aftermarket scan). The 10.75 mm figure is arithmetic only.
   Settles: the deck thickness to aim for, and whether the genuine unit matches the aftermarket scan.
3. **Screen frame height and rise.**
   How: (a) back face to the top of the raised frame at its front edge, about 25 mm right of centre (clear of the Slot-2 finger notch), jaws in about 9 mm; (b) the same at the frame's right side, jaws passing between the B button and START (about 27 mm from the front edge); (c) the width of the sloped band between the flat wing and the flat frame top, measured across the face with the jaw tips. The rise is 3b minus 2c.
   Dossier now: frame top 15.06 (sides) and 15.17 (front) mm, rise 1.8 to 2.3 mm on a concave cove about 7 mm wide (aftermarket scan).
   Settles: the step between our screen block and the button decks, and its slope.
4. **Cap height above the face.**
   How: (a) back face to the top of the A cap, minus back face to the face plate right beside it, with the lower jaw on the same spot of the back both times so any unevenness cancels (use the narrow jaw tips); (b) the same for the D-pad at its centre; (c) the same at the tip of the D-pad's left arm.
   Dossier now: ABXY about 1.3 mm (Pi Tin, a printed shell for genuine caps) against about 0.4 mm (inferred in DSLITE.md from the aftermarket scan). No genuine measurement exists.
   Settles: `cap_proud`, and so the deck height and the `dome_h` estimate.
5. **Balance points.**
   How: lay the closed unit face up on a round pencil, slide until it balances, mark the pencil line at the edge with tape, and measure the distance with the calipers. (a) Pencil running front to back: distance from the left end. (b) Pencil running left to right: distance from the front edge. (c) Open the lid to its last detent, rest the bottom half on the pencil running left to right: distance from the front edge.
   Dossier now: no measurement anywhere. Nintendo says it balanced front/back and left/right; the battery sits behind the left thumb.
   Settles: where ballast should go in our case, and how far the open lid pulls the balance toward the hinge.
6. **Corner and edge radii.**
   How: (a) Coin test: lay a 1 euro cent (16.25 mm, r 8.1) or a US dime (17.9 mm, r 9.0) flat on the back at a front corner, its rim lined up with both straight edges; say whether plastic shows beyond the coin at the corner (the corner is tighter than the coin) or the coin overhangs (looser). Repeat with the flat end of a AAA cell (r 5.25). (b) Photograph a front corner straight down from 30 cm or more on squared paper; the radius is the distance from the imaginary sharp corner to where each edge starts to curve. (c) Photograph the left end of the closed unit straight on against squared paper to show the curve from the back face up into the front wall; send the photos if easier.
   Dossier now: plan corner 4.29 to 4.42 mm, back-edge blend 4.18 to 4.23 mm (aftermarket scan); top-half corner 3.8 to 4.0 mm. case.scad: 8 mm corners, square back edge.
   Settles: `corner_r` and whether to add a back-edge fillet.
7. **ABXY centre-to-centre spacing.**
   How: (a) outside jaws across the far sides of the Y and A caps; (b) inside jaws between the facing sides of Y and A. Pitch = (a + b) / 2, cap diameter = (a minus b) / 2. Repeat for X and B.
   Dossier now: 15.0 (two PCB projects), 15.14 and 15.22 (genuine board redraw), 15.2 to 15.3 (patent), 15.35 (aftermarket shell holes) mm.
   Settles: the lid's hole pitch.
8. **Cap sizes.**
   How: outside jaws on the part of each cap above the face. (a) A cap diameter (also from item 7); (b) D-pad tip to tip, left to right and up to down; (c) D-pad arm width; (d) START cap diameter, and START to SELECT spacing by the item 7 method.
   Dossier now: ABXY 6.94 to 7.00 mm, D-pad 17.5 to 17.8 x 5.8 to 5.9 mm (replica models), START/SELECT 3.4 mm (replica) and 7.7 mm apart (patent, board).
   Settles: hole and D-pad clearances, and whether bought caps match genuine ones. The holes themselves cannot be measured with the caps in place, and the caps only come out with the shell open, so the hole diameter is not on this list.
9. **Battery and material (optional, one screw).**
   How: undo the captive screw, lift the cover, ease the battery out with a plastic card (not metal). (a) Battery mass; (b) cover mass; (c) unit mass without battery and cover; (d) repeat balance 5a without the battery; (e) any moulded text in the bay floor and inside the cover (for example ">ABS<" or ">PC+ABS<"); (f) the capacity on the battery label; (g) battery length, width and thickness.
   Dossier now: no genuine battery mass (estimate 20 to 25 g; vendors 20 to 45 g); 1000 mAh, 3.7 Wh; ">ABS,PC+ABS<" seen in one iFixit photo.
   Settles: the left-right mass bias and the shell material.
10. **Top half and closed thickness.**
    How: (a) closed thickness at a front corner; (b) the lid alone at its free-end edge, lid open; (c) closed thickness at the left hinge knuckle.
    Dossier now: 21.5 mm closed (official); top half about 7.9 mm, knuckle 21.2 mm (aftermarket scan).
    Settles: confirms the 13.5 plus 7.9 split with item 2.
11. **Back flatness.**
    How: stand the caliper beam (or a steel rule) on its edge across the back, corner to corner both ways, and look for light under the middle or under the ends; count how many sheets of printer paper (about 0.1 mm each) slide into the biggest gap.
    Dossier now: the scan's centre is 0.4 to 0.6 mm higher than its edges, probably warp.
    Settles: a flat or slightly crowned tub floor.

## What this means for our case

From case.scad: the case is 75.4 x 156.24 mm with 8 mm plan corners, 14.6 mm thick over the screen and 11.06 mm at the button decks (a 3.54 mm step with a 2 mm, 45 degree chamfer), with 2.0 mm walls, a 1.5 mm floor and a 2.0 mm lid; the tub is a straight extrusion, so its back edge is square; the lid sits on top of the tub wall, so the seam runs around the side 2.0 mm below the face; `cap_hole_d` is 7.6, the D-pad gap 0.5 and `piece_d` 26. These are recommendations, highest leverage first, not decisions.

1. **Match the density.** The envelope is about 157 cm³ (derived from case.scad), so 1.03 g/cm³ means about 160 g (177 g on the bounding box). Weigh the built unit; if it is short, add a flat steel plate low and central rather than at the ends. PLA's higher density helps.
2. **Round the back edge and tighten the corners.** A 4 mm blend from the back into the walls is the biggest tactile difference; on a floor-down FDM print, finish its lowest millimetre as a 45 degree chamfer. Consider 4.3 mm plan corners; case.scad derives `board_r` from `corner_r`, so decouple them first.
3. **Move the seam to the face.** Nest the lid inside a 0.6 to 0.8 mm tub lip, as the DS Lite does, so the sides are one surface and the seam sits where gloss meets matte. Gloss the tub (sanded colour base under a 2K clear); leave the lid's face matte.
4. **Close the deck gap.** The screen block is within 0.5 to 1.0 mm of the DS Lite's frame top, but the decks are 2.0 to 2.5 mm thinner than its wings and the step is 3.54 mm against 1.8 to 2.3. If item 4 shows genuine caps stand lower than 1.3 mm, raise the decks by the difference at no cost. Replacing the vertical step wall and its 45 degree chamfer with a concave cove tangent to the deck would echo the DS Lite's frame (Derived from case.scad: the screen block's flat face ends 43.52 mm from centre and the nearest ABXY hole starts at 49.82). A cove of radius about 10.8 mm over an 8 mm run fits our 3.54 mm step: 48 degrees at the crest; it follows the existing chamfer to within 0.03 mm for the first millimetre and stays above it after that, so it only adds material in the corner on the deck; and 0.13 mm high where it crosses the hole edge, as the DS Lite's Y hole sits on its cove's tail. The DS Lite's own 30 to 33 degrees needs a step near 2 mm.
5. **Keep the control details.** Keep the 7.6 mm holes and 0.5 mm D-pad gap; keep the board bare and flat under the D-pad's peg; pocket the lid over the ABXY pad's raised centre diamond; raise `piece_d` to about 27; make sure no flange touches the lid at rest.
6. **Measure items 1 to 5 before changing geometry.**
