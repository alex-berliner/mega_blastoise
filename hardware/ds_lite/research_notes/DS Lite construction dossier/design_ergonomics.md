# Nintendo DS Lite (USG-001): design intent, design language and ergonomics

Scope: the "why" and the human experience of the DS Lite, with emphasis on the bottom half held in the hands. Raw dimensions, materials, internals and switch mechanics belong to other researchers; numbers appear here only where they explain intent or feel.

Confidence labels used on every finding:
- **official**: Nintendo or Nintendo staff (press releases, Nintendo's own magazine N.O.M, Iwata Asks, Nintendo's Good Design Award submission).
- **reviewer opinion**: a named press outlet or reviewer (includes their factual observations of a retail unit).
- **community opinion**: forum posts, reader comments, Reddit, maker pages.
- **inference**: my own reasoning from the cited material.

Source handling notes:
- Several English launch reviews survive only as mirrors (CNET and Trusted Reviews on retroisle.com) or Wayback captures (Ars Technica). The mirrors are labelled.
- Items marked "(search excerpt only)" were seen in a search result snippet but the page itself could not be fetched; treat them as weaker.
- Model conflation flags: the English Wikipedia "Nintendo DS Lite" title now redirects into the general Nintendo DS article, so DS, DS Lite, DSi figures sit side by side there. Inverse (2024) wrongly describes the DS Lite as having "3.12-inch" screens and a "capacitive touch sensor" (it has 3.0 inch screens and a resistive panel); its design commentary is used here but its specs are not.

## 1. Design history: who designed it, and what were the goals?

### Takeaway
The DS Lite's industrial designer was Yui Ehara (江原唯), working under design manager Kenichi Sugino and producer Satoru Okada in Nintendo's Development Engineering (開発技術部) design group, with mechanical design by Kazuo Yoneyama and Tomoyuki Sakiyama. Nintendo's own stated goals were: make the DS smaller, thinner and lighter for portability without sacrificing operability (same screen size, same button layout), make it brighter, make a multi-function machine look simple and non-intimidating to beginners, women and older people, support landscape, portrait and one-handed holds, and give it a premium "object" quality through a deliberate glossy-outside, matte-inside texture contrast. Apple/iPod comparisons came from the press, not from Nintendo.

**Stated goals (official)**
- [official] Press release, 26 Jan 2006: the DS Lite is an upper model (上位モデル) that makes the body thinner and lighter to raise portability (携帯性を高める) and adds a four-step brightness adjustment. [Nintendo news release, 2006-01-26](https://www.nintendo.co.jp/corporate/release/2006/060126.html)
- [official] Iwata at the Feb 15, 2006 DS conference: "ボディは薄く、小さくなりましたが、画面の大きさは変わりません" ("the body is thinner and smaller, but the screen size has not changed"), and the best way to appreciate it is "直接触っていただくのが一番" ("touching it directly is best"). [GAME Watch, 15 Feb 2006](https://game.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/20060215/nin3.htm)
- [official] Good Design Award "design points" (デザインのポイント), Nintendo's own words, three bullets:
  1. "多機能ながら、ゲーム初心者にそれが「複雑で難しいもの」と錯覚されないようなレイアウトと配色" (a layout and colour scheme such that, although multi-functional, game beginners do not mistake it for something "complicated and difficult").
  2. "横持ち、縦持ち、片手持ちなど様々な使用形態での使いやすさを想定したバランスと形状" (balance and shape designed for ease of use in many holds: landscape, portrait, one-handed).
  3. "質感にコントラストを与えモノとしての高級感とゲームソフトへの手触り感を演出するスタイリッシュなデザイン" (a stylish design that gives contrast to the surface textures, producing a sense of luxury as an object and a hands-on, touchable feel that leads into the game software). [Good Design Award 2006](https://www.g-mark.org/gallery/winners/9d46dc0b-803d-11ed-862b-0242ac130002)
- [official] Designer's statement in the same entry: the aim was a DS that people of any age, gender, nationality or gaming experience could enjoy from "the same starting line" (同じスタートライン), so that beginners could play "安心して手軽に直感的に" (with peace of mind, casually, intuitively); "ゲームソフト開発のノウハウを注ぎ込み、携帯性と操作性の最高のバランスを実現" (pouring in game-software development know-how to achieve the best balance of portability and operability), while improving long-term reliability. Usability work: they verified the operation flow so a game can be started smoothly, and tested the operation keys "aiming for the best balance usable by people all over the world". Durability: "1.5メートルからの厳しい落下試験" (strict drop tests from 1.5 m), designed to be "hard to break" and to "break in a safe way if it does break"; key tops tested to 5 million presses. [Good Design Award 2006](https://www.g-mark.org/gallery/winners/9d46dc0b-803d-11ed-862b-0242ac130002)
- [official] Ehara's N.O.M message: DS Lite was designed for a broad audience regardless of age, gender or gaming experience; "DSは多機能ではありますが、それが「複雑で難しいもの」と錯覚されないよう、いろいろな工夫をしています" (the DS has many functions, but we made various efforts so it is not mistaken for something complicated and difficult); designed to be played "手軽に、直感的に" (casually, intuitively). [N.O.M 4-2](http://web.archive.org/web/20060405020918/http://www.nintendo.co.jp:80/nom/0604/42/index.html)
- [official] Yoneyama (N.O.M): "携帯型ゲーム機として、操作性を犠牲にしてまで小さくするわけにはいかないんです。もちろんディスプレイの大きさはキープしたいし、ボタンの配置も変えるわけにはいかない" (as a handheld you cannot shrink it at the cost of operability; we wanted to keep the display size and could not change the button layout). Custom parts were made to hit the size: a thinner but stronger custom top LCD module and custom stereo speakers. [N.O.M 4-2](http://web.archive.org/web/20060405020918/http://www.nintendo.co.jp:80/nom/0604/42/index.html)
- [official] Yoneyama (N.O.M) on internal layout: parts are laid out "ユーザーさんが手に持ったときの前後左右の重心バランスも考えながら" (while considering the front/back/left/right centre-of-gravity balance when the user holds it). [N.O.M 4-2](http://web.archive.org/web/20060405020918/http://www.nintendo.co.jp:80/nom/0604/42/index.html)
- [official] Yoneyama (N.O.M) on strength: they raised strength considerably via internal reinforcement and part placement and ran long strength tests, "ただ、そこには重さやサイズとの葛藤もある" (but there is a conflict with weight and size). [N.O.M 4-2](http://web.archive.org/web/20060405020918/http://www.nintendo.co.jp:80/nom/0604/42/index.html)
- [official] Size change as Nintendo framed it (N.O.M 4-1): 15.7 mm narrower, 10.8 mm shorter, 7.4 mm thinner when closed; volume only 58% of the DS; 57 g lighter. (These deltas reproduce 133.0 x 73.9 x 21.5 mm and 218 g from the DS's 148.7 x 84.7 x 28.9 mm and 275 g.) [N.O.M 4-1, Wayback](http://web.archive.org/web/20060405020911/http://www.nintendo.co.jp:80/nom/0604/41/index.html)
- [community opinion] Name: "Lite" is said to mean both 軽い (light in weight) and 明るい (bright). [pixiv encyclopedia](https://dic.pixiv.net/a/DSLite). I could not verify this wording in the Nintendo press release because its text was mis-encoded when fetched. [official] Yoneyama adds that the colours were chosen partly so buyers would get a "LIGHT" image in the sense of lightness. [N.O.M 4-2](http://web.archive.org/web/20060405020918/http://www.nintendo.co.jp:80/nom/0604/42/index.html)

### Inferences
- [inference] Nintendo's own documents never cite Apple. The stated intent is accessibility (look simple, not "complicated and difficult"), portability without losing operability, multi-grip usability, and a premium "object" feel created by texture contrast. The Apple reading is the press's interpretation of a white, glossy, clear-coated, minimal slab in 2006, when the white iPod and iBook/MacBook defined that vocabulary. A replica that hits the press cues (white gloss, clean lines) will read as "DS Lite"; one that also hits Nintendo's cues (texture contrast, balanced weight, uncluttered layout) will feel like one.
- [inference] Mechanical design was explicitly constrained to keep the original DS's screen size and button layout, so the DS Lite's identity is a refinement of proportions, surfaces and feel rather than a new layout.

## 2. Design language and visual identity: what makes it read as "DS Lite"?

### Takeaway
The DS Lite reads as two near-identical flat rectangles with rounded corners, glossy outside and matte inside, with almost no surface graphics and no visible gaps or bits sticking out. Nintendo's designer describes the DS family identity as an icon "two rectangles, top and bottom, with each half containing another rectangle inside". The two textures (glass-like clear-over-colour gloss outside, matte inner face around the controls) and the white colourway are what reviewers noticed first. Compared with the original DS it lost the two-tone, "underbite" bottom-heavy look; the DSi kept the form but went matte and changed the speakers; the 3DS deliberately broke from the Lite's language.

### Cited Findings

**Form and proportion**
- [official] N.O.M hands-on (Nintendo's magazine): "長方形を基調にしたフラットなデザインがよりコンパクトさを感じさせる" (the flat design based on the rectangle makes it feel even more compact). [N.O.M 4-1](http://web.archive.org/web/20060405020911/http://www.nintendo.co.jp:80/nom/0604/41/index.html)
- [official] Ehara (Iwata Asks DSi, 2008, talking about the DS line he shaped from the Lite onwards): "I want to make Nintendo DS a kind of icon... two rectangles, top and bottom, with each half containing another rectangle inside... that even an elementary schooler who isn't very good at drawing could draw so that anyone would recognize it and say, 'Oh, that's a DS!'"; "The console exists for the software, so I designed it not to stand out too much"; the aim was for it to "melt seamlessly into the customers' lives". [Iwata Asks: Nintendo DSi Vol. 1 ch. 3](https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/ds/dsi/0/2/)
- [reviewer opinion] CNET: the top and bottom halves are now symmetrical, avoiding the original's "underbite-like look" from its larger bottom half. [CNET mirror](https://www.retroisle.com/others/nintendods/Articles/dslite_review_cnet.php)
- [reviewer opinion] Ars Technica: "smaller, sleeker, and the lines are more modern than the odd angles of the first DS"; the original "looked like something you kept makeup in". [Ars p.1, Wayback](http://web.archive.org/web/20060616/http://arstechnica.com:80/reviews/hardware/dslite.ars)
- [reviewer opinion] Inverse: "Where the DS had visible seams and thick sides, the Nintendo DS Lite was slimmer, smoother, and overall cleaner"; credits its "rounded corners and a simple design that was not common for game systems yet". [Inverse](https://inverse.com/tech/nintendo-ds-lite-18-year-anniversary)
- [reviewer opinion] Nintendo Life: original DS had a "dumpy, two-tone casing"; the Lite is "one of the most beautiful pieces of hardware Nintendo has ever created", praising its "sleek, pocket-sized dimensions" and "glossy exterior and matte-finished interior". [Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2013/02/hardware_classics_nintendo_ds_lite)

**Surface finish: gloss outside, matte inside (the key texture contrast)**
- [official] N.O.M hands-on: "本体の表面はガラスのカバーをかぶせたような光沢を持っています。それとは反対に十字ボタンやＡ・Ｂ・Ｘ・Ｙボタンの周囲はつや消し処理がしてあって、本体の内と外における２種類の質感の対比が独特の高級感を演出しています。もはやゲーム機の領域を越えているかも。" (The body surface has a gloss as if covered with glass. In contrast, the area around the + Control Pad and A/B/X/Y buttons has a matte treatment, and the contrast of two textures, inside and outside, creates a unique premium feel. It may already be beyond the realm of game machines.) [N.O.M 4-1](http://web.archive.org/web/20060405020911/http://www.nintendo.co.jp:80/nom/0604/41/index.html)
- [official] Good Design Award design point 3: "質感にコントラストを与え" (giving contrast to the textures) to create premium object quality. [Good Design Award 2006](https://www.g-mark.org/gallery/winners/9d46dc0b-803d-11ed-862b-0242ac130002)
- [official] Sakiyama (N.O.M): "今回は２色成形で独特な質感を出しています。クリスタルホワイトとアイスブルーに関しては透明な部品を、エナメルネイビーには薄いブルーがかった半透明の部品が使われています" (this time we used two-colour moulding to create a unique texture; Crystal White and Ice Blue use a transparent part, Enamel Navy a pale-blue tinted translucent part). Colour-check prototypes, normally made in-house, were outsourced to a specialist because of the two-shot process: "それぐらいこだわりのある微妙な質感" (that is how particular we were about the subtle texture). [N.O.M 4-2](http://web.archive.org/web/20060405020918/http://www.nintendo.co.jp:80/nom/0604/42/index.html)
- [reviewer opinion] GAME Watch (Feb 2006 announcement report): colour names suggest gloss but finishes differ; white "透明感のある素材に白色が映える作り" (white set off by a translucent material); navy "しっとりした藍色" (a moist, deep indigo). [GAME Watch, 10 Feb 2006](https://game.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/20060210/dslite.htm)
- [reviewer opinion] Shacknews: outside "smooth and shiny plastic similar to that used in the PSP"; inside "a matte finish, somewhat like the plastic used on the bottom of the original DS". [Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/41445/nintendo-ds-lite-enamel-navy)
- [reviewer opinion] Nintendo World Report review (Jonathan Metts, 27 Apr 2006): shell "very shiny and flat"; "the attractive outer shell does not extend to the inside surfaces. The matte finish inside is even off-tone, so the colors don't match"; the fold edges "appear to be yet another, lighter color"; author follow-up: "All three colors have different outside and inside tones." [NWR review](https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/11365)

**How the Lite differs from DS, DSi, 3DS (only where it defines the Lite)**

### Inferences
- [inference] Most recognisable, in order: (1) the white glossy flat slab with rounded plan corners; (2) the symmetric clamshell with the hinge barrel as a raised cylinder along the top of the bottom half; (3) the gloss-outside / matte-inside contrast; (4) a near-blank surface with only small stencilled labels, which appear to float under the clear layer on white units.
- [inference] For a bottom-half replica the "rectangle inside a rectangle" reading comes from the touch screen window centred in the flat inner face, flanked by the D-pad (left) and ABXY (right). Keeping that inner face flat, matte and uncluttered matters more than reproducing every label.
- [inference] Gloss "depth" is the subtle tell: Nintendo used a clear or translucent shot over the colour, so the colour sits slightly below a glassy skin and the edge band reads lighter (NWR's "lighter color" fold edges). A painted or single-shot glossy white will look flatter.

- I could not confirm the location of the "POWER" stencil GAME Watch mentions, or whether Western Polar White is physically identical to Japanese Crystal White (it is very likely the same part, but unconfirmed).

- Apparent tension: CNET lists Crimson and Cobalt as red/blue "and black" DS Lites, while Fujino says two-tone "didn't work very well" on the DS Lite. [inference] Crimson/Cobalt use a coloured exterior with black inner surfaces, so Fujino likely meant a different kind of two-tone split; unverified.

## 3. Ergonomics: how people hold the open DS Lite

### Takeaway
Nintendo designed the shape and balance for landscape, portrait and one-handed holds, and moved the microphone and power switch for those holds. In landscape play the bottom half is not wrapped by the palms: reviewers describe cradling it on the fingertips with thumbs on the D-pad and ABXY and index fingers on low-profile L/R. People with moderate hands found it comfortable after a short adjustment; larger-handed adults reported cramping because the thin, small body cannot be gripped tightly and the buttons are small and close together.

### Inferences
- [inference] Landscape grip: with the lid open the top half stands behind the hinge, so the hands hold only the bottom half. At about 133 mm wide, 73.9 mm deep and about 10.75 mm thick (half of the 21.5 mm closed thickness), the half is too thin to fill a palm. The weight is carried on the fingertips (middle and ring fingers) under the two lower corners, thumbs over the D-pad and ABXY, and index fingers on the rear corners where L/R sit. This matches the "fingertips" descriptions in NWR and Ars and explains the cramping complaints: the hand has nothing to close around.
- [inference] Contact edges in landscape: the bottom-left and bottom-right corners and the near (front) long edge rest against the fingers and the base of the thumbs, the rear corners sit under the index fingers, and the hinge barrel is not touched. Edge comfort therefore depends mostly on the front edge and the two front corners; I found no complaint that those edges are sharp.
- [inference] Stylus play: one hand holds the bottom half (or the device rests on the fingers of one hand) while the other writes, which reviewers say is the most comfortable mode. Portrait (book) play uses the lid recess as a thumb rest.

### Gaps
- No source measures or describes the D-pad's distance from the left edge, or the ABXY distance from the right edge; defer to the dimensions researcher.
- No source found that describes the DS Lite's perimeter edges as sharp or uncomfortable, or discusses the hand-contact edges specifically.
- No Japanese-language user comments on DS Lite hand fatigue were found.
- No data on child vs adult hand fit specific to the DS Lite beyond "large-handed" complaints.

## 5. Criticisms of the design and how successors responded

### Takeaway
The main criticisms were the fingerprint-magnet gloss (worst on dark colours), hinge cracking near the L button and lamps, protruding GBA cartridges, small and stiff Start/Select, small buttons and cramping for large hands, the off-tone matte interior, and weak speakers. The DSi answered with a matte exterior, louder sound, slot speaker grilles, larger screens, a slightly lighter body and no GBA slot, while keeping the overall Lite form almost unchanged.

## 6. Handheld ergonomics research relevant to a ~73 mm deep, ~10 to 11 mm thick glossy base

### Takeaway
I found no published study of two-handed game handheld grip thickness, width or edge radius. The closest peer-reviewed work is on one-handed smartphone grip (width matters most; about 8 mm thick, 2.5 mm edge roundness and about 106 to 137 g rated best) and thumb reach envelopes for handheld control placement. These support, by analogy only, that a thin, flat slab with small edge radii can be comfortable when the controls sit inside the thumbs' primary reach zones, and they help explain why the DS Lite is supported on the fingertips rather than gripped.

### Cited Findings
- [official, peer-reviewed] Lee et al., "Determining Ergonomic Smartphone Forms With High Grip Comfort and Attractive Design", Human Factors 2019 (72 young adults, three stages): width had the strongest effect on grip comfort and design attractiveness; 140 x 65 (or 70) x 8 mm with 2.5 mm edge roundness gave high comfort and attractiveness; a mass of 122 g fitted best, with 106 to 137 g comparable. [DOI 10.1177/0018720818792758](https://doi.org/10.1177/0018720818792758) (PMID 30085850)
- [official, peer-reviewed] Otten, Karn and Parsons, "Defining thumb reach envelopes for handheld devices", Human Factors 2013 (90 participants: adolescent girls, young mothers, young fathers): presents 2D thumb reach envelopes for primary control surfaces of devices including game systems; "the preferred location for controls matches the primary thumb reach zones fairly well"; adolescent girls' primary zone is slightly larger and more circular than adults'. [DOI 10.1177/0018720812470689](https://doi.org/10.1177/0018720812470689) (PMID 23516793)
- [official] Nintendo's own process: the operation keys were tested "to aim for the best balance usable by people around the world", and part layout considered the in-hand centre of gravity. [Good Design Award 2006](https://www.g-mark.org/gallery/winners/9d46dc0b-803d-11ed-862b-0242ac130002), [N.O.M 4-2](http://web.archive.org/web/20060405020918/http://www.nintendo.co.jp:80/nom/0604/42/index.html)

### Inferences
- [inference] The DS Lite bottom half (about 10.75 mm) is only slightly thicker than the 8 mm smartphone optimum, so it is far below the size that palms can close around. Comfort then depends on light mass (218 g total, spread across two hands, so about 109 g per hand, right in Lee et al.'s comfortable band), small but not sharp edge radii where fingers press, and controls inside the thumb's natural arc. This is consistent with reviewers' "fingertip cradle" description and with large-handed users cramping.
- [inference] Gloss vs grip: no study found, but a high-gloss hard plastic has low friction when dry; on a fingertip-supported device this is partly offset by the light mass. A replica that is heavier than about 220 g would make the slippery gloss more noticeable.

### Gaps
- No study of two-handed landscape handheld console grip, of edge radius alone, or of gloss vs matte grip friction for handhelds was found. A 2020 Human Factors study on rollable-display thickness (2, 6, 10 mm) surfaced in search but I could not verify its details, so it is not used.
- No r/SBCGaming or retro-handheld reviewer measurements of DS Lite ergonomics were found.

## 7. Final checklist: cues a replica bottom half must get right, ordered by importance

### Takeaway
To read and feel like a DS Lite bottom half, the replica must first be a flat, crisp, rounded-corner rectangle of the right footprint and thickness, glossy (ideally clear-over-colour) on the outside and matte on the inner control face, with an uncluttered surface and no gaps. Second, the controls must sit where the DS put them, with the Lite's specific forms (raised soft D-pad, domed ABXY, two small round Start/Select under ABXY, low L/R hugging the rear corners, firm side power slider). Third, it must weigh and balance like a dense ~218 g object and the lid must snap shut firmly.

### Cited Findings
Ordered checklist, each with evidence and confidence.

1. **Flat rectangular slab, symmetric with the lid, rounded plan corners, right proportions.** Footprint 133.0 x 73.9 mm, closed 21.5 mm (so about 10.75 mm per half); the halves match so there is no "underbite". Evidence: N.O.M "長方形を基調にしたフラットなデザイン" [official] [N.O.M 4-1](http://web.archive.org/web/20060405020911/http://www.nintendo.co.jp:80/nom/0604/41/index.html); Ehara's "two rectangles... each half containing another rectangle" icon [official] [Iwata Asks DSi](https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/ds/dsi/0/2/); CNET symmetry/"underbite" [reviewer opinion] [CNET mirror](https://www.retroisle.com/others/nintendods/Articles/dslite_review_cnet.php); Inverse "rounded corners" [reviewer opinion] [Inverse](https://inverse.com/tech/nintendo-ds-lite-18-year-anniversary).
2. **Gloss outside, matte inside (texture contrast).** Outer surfaces high gloss "as if covered with glass"; the inner face around the D-pad and ABXY matte, and slightly off-tone from the exterior. Evidence: N.O.M 4-1 and Good Design design point 3 [official]; NWR, Shacknews, Nintendo Life [reviewer opinion] (all cited in section 2).
3. **Gloss with depth: colour under a clear or translucent skin.** Two-shot moulding with a transparent part (white, ice blue) or pale-blue translucent part (navy); labels appear to float; the shell edge reads as a lighter band. Evidence: Sakiyama [official] [N.O.M 4-2](http://web.archive.org/web/20060405020918/http://www.nintendo.co.jp:80/nom/0604/42/index.html); GAME Watch "iBook" note [reviewer opinion] [GAME Watch](https://game.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/20060215/nin3.htm); NWR "lighter color" fold edges [reviewer opinion] [NWR review](https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/11365). Which bottom-half surfaces get this treatment is unconfirmed (see Gaps).
4. **Clean, unbroken surfaces and minimal graphics.** No gaps when closed; slot blanked by a dummy cart; small charge port; stylus flush and held by a broad clip; only small stencilled labels; no Nintendo badge on the lid (embossed double-rectangle). Evidence: Ars, Shacknews, Trusted, GAME Watch, NWR [reviewer opinion] (section 2).
5. **Colour: white first.** Crystal White (Japan) / Polar White (West) is the colourway the press tied to the Apple look; the launch palette was chosen to be easy for women and older users and to say "light". Evidence: N.O.M [official]; Macworld, Ars, Trusted, CNN excerpt [reviewer opinion] (sections 1 and 2).
6. **Control placement unchanged from DS, with Lite details.** Touch screen window centred in the inner face; D-pad left and ABXY right, both sitting slightly higher/proud than on the DS; Start and Select as two small round buttons below ABXY on the right; power as a spring-loaded slider on the right edge near the stylus slot, needing firm force; L/R low-profile, hugging the rear corners. Evidence: Yoneyama "ボタンの配置も変えるわけにはいかない" [official]; ja.wikipedia [encyclopedia]; Shacknews, NWR, Ars, CNET, GAME Watch [reviewer opinion] (sections 2 to 4).
7. **Hinge barrel along the rear edge of the bottom half carrying the lamps (right) and microphone (centre).** Evidence: GAME Watch, CNET [reviewer opinion]; Miyatake "all lined up on the hinge" [official] [Iwata Asks 3DS](https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/3ds/how-nintendo-3ds-made/2/4/); Yoneyama mic rationale [official].
8. **Weight and balance: dense, centred.** 218 g total, centre of gravity balanced front/back/left/right. Evidence: N.O.M [official]; Ars "far more dense and satisfying" [reviewer opinion].
9. **Button feel: soft, raised, quiet main controls; crisp shoulders.** D-pad raised, softer and more tactile, less clicky than DS; ABXY deeper and softer (Nintendo: "close to the GameCube controller"); L/R reassuring click; Start/Select stiff. Evidence: Yoneyama [official]; Shacknews, NWR, Trusted, Nintendo Life, Ars, GAME Watch [reviewer opinion] (section 4).
10. **Lid action and sound: firm snap shut, slight detent click.** Evidence: NWR "snaps shut firm and tight" [reviewer opinion]; Reddit "slight click when the hinge locked" [community opinion, excerpt] (section 4).
11. **Edge feel: smooth, not sharp, around the front corners where fingers support it.** Evidence is indirect: reviewers describe fingertip support and smooth surfaces; nobody complains of sharp edges (sections 3 and 4) [inference from absence].

### Inferences
- [inference] Priority logic: items 1 to 5 are what make it read as a DS Lite at a glance in photos (form, gloss, contrast, cleanliness, white). Items 6 to 9 are what make it feel like one in use. Items 10 and 11 are refinements that people notice only when they are wrong.
- [inference] The single most commonly missed cue in replicas will be item 2 (matte inner face). An all-gloss or all-matte part loses the "two-texture" premium that Nintendo itself named as a design point.
- [inference] Do not add speaker holes to the bottom half: the DS Lite's two speaker hole groups are on the top half, flanking the upper screen.

### Gaps
- Exact edge and corner radii, surface gloss level (GU), texture spec of the matte inner face, and actual colour values (Crystal White vs Polar White) are not in any design-intent source; defer to the materials and dimensions researchers.
- No source confirms whether the matte area covers the entire inner face of the bottom half or only the zones around the controls; N.O.M says "around" the D-pad and ABXY, other reviewers say "inside" generally.
