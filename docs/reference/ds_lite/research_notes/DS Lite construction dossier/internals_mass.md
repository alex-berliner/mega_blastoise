# Nintendo DS Lite (USG-001): internal construction, assembly and mass distribution (bottom half focus)

Scope: what is physically inside the bottom half, how it is fastened and stacked, what each part weighs (where anyone has published it), and why the result feels solid; plus the top half and hinge facts that change how the bottom half feels in the hand. DS Lite (USG-001) only; other models appear only as labelled comparisons.

Confidence labels used on every number or claim:
- **[official]**: Nintendo (spec pages, press release, product label, Nintendo staff interview, FCC filing by Nintendo).
- **[measured: who]**: a third party physically measured it (for example iFixit's screw lengths).
- **[observed]**: my own reading of a photo (FCC internal photo exhibits, iFixit step photos). No copying; facts only.
- **[vendor claim]**: an aftermarket seller's listing. Not the Nintendo part.
- **[community]**: forum, Reddit, maker or repair-guide author opinion or anecdote.
- **[derived]**: arithmetic on cited numbers, with the arithmetic shown.
- **[estimated]**: my engineering estimate with stated assumptions. Not a measurement.
- **(search excerpt only)**: seen in a search result snippet; the page itself could not be fetched (403 or 503). Weaker.

Naming trap (important when reading iFixit): iFixit calls the bottom half's **back** shell (battery side) the "lower case" and the bottom half's **front** shell (button and touchscreen face) the "upper case". The top half's shells are the "front display bezel" and "rear display bezel". I use plain words below: **front shell** = button face of the bottom half, **back shell** = battery side of the bottom half.

Model conflation flags:
- The GameSpot English summary of Nintendo's April 2006 engineer interview says "the bottom one is the same as the display in the DS". The Japanese original says only that the original DS used the same LCD top and bottom, and that the DS Lite's **upper** LCD is a different, custom part. Nintendo's spec page lists both DS Lite LCDs as transmissive while the original DS's were transflective, so the DS Lite lower LCD is not literally the original DS part. Treat the GameSpot wording as a translation simplification.
- iFixit teardown 73109 is a 5 step student teardown with an estimated time of "3 to 5 minutes"; low detail, used only for corroboration. iFixit's Device page itself states that repair steps differ across DS, DS Lite and DSi and identifies the DS Lite by model number USG-001 on the back label.
- The FCC exhibit photos (filed 2006-02-03) show a pre-production unit whose mainboard silkscreen reads **C/USG-CPU-X3** (a prototype revision), not a retail board. Retail units may differ in details (Wi-Fi module part, flex stiffness, adhesive).

## 1. Full parts list of the bottom half (and which parts live in the top half)

### Takeaway
The bottom half holds almost everything heavy: the mainboard with the Slot-1 (DS card) cage, the long Slot-2 (GBA) connector, the shielded Wi-Fi module, headphone jack, volume slider, DC jack and power slide switch; the lower LCD with its separate resistive touch panel; the 3.7 V 1000 mAh USG-003 battery in a bay in the back shell; the stylus in a screwed-in silo; rubber-dome button membranes and caps; and the spring-loaded L/R buttons. The speakers, microphone, Wi-Fi antenna and upper LCD are all in the top half, connected to the bottom through the hinge by one flex cable plus two thin wires (black antenna coax and white mic wire).

### Cited Findings

**Shells and trim**
- Front shell (button face, with LCD window, power and charge LED windows next to the hinge) and back shell (battery bay, Slot-1 and Slot-2 openings, rubber feet) are separate mouldings; the back shell is removed first after 7 screws ([iFixit Lower Case Replacement, guide 4636](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Lower+Case+Replacement/4636)) [measured: iFixit].
- Power and volume slide switch **covers** (caps) belong to the back shell and must be aligned with the switches on the board when refitting ([iFixit guide 1922 step 7](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Disassembling+Nintendo+DS+Lite+Bottom+Case/1922); [guide 4636 step 6](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Lower+Case+Replacement/4636)) [measured: iFixit].
- Power LED moved to the right of the hinge with the charge LED next to it; on the original DS they were below the lower screen ([Game Watch, 2006-02-10](https://game.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/20060210/dslite.htm)) [official, reported].
- The outer cosmetic faces are two-shot (2色成形) mouldings: Crystal White and Ice Blue use a transparent layer, Enamel Navy a thin blue-tinted translucent layer (Sakiyama, [N.O.M April 2006 staff interview, Wayback](http://web.archive.org/web/20060405020918/http://www.nintendo.co.jp:80/nom/0604/42/index.html)) [official]. For Crystal White, the glossy clear parts are the outer face of the lid and the outer face of the bottom half's back; the rest is matte white ([Game Watch, 2006-03-06](https://game.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/20060306/dsl.htm)) [official product, reviewer observation].
- The back shell's battery bay floor carries the moulded material code ">ABS,PC+ABS<" and the text "3.7V USG-003" (iFixit guide 4636 step 4 photo, black unit) ([guide 4636](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Lower+Case+Replacement/4636)) [observed].
- GBA connector cover USG-005 ships inserted in Slot-2, made of the same plastic as the shell ([Game Watch, 2006-03-06](https://game.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/20060306/dsl.htm); [Nintendo World Report review](https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/11365)) [official part number; reviewer].

**Mainboard**
- FCC sample board silkscreen: "C/USG-CPU-X3", with the Nintendo logo ([FCC ID BKEUSG-001, Internal Photos 1](https://fccid.io/BKEUSG-001/Internal-Photos/Internal-Photos-1-626991)) [observed].
- Retail boards are reported as "C/USG-CPU-01" and "C/USG-CPU-10" ([GBAtemp thread "differences between DS Lite mobos"](https://gbatemp.net/threads/differences-between-ds-lite-mobos.481192/)) [community] (search excerpt only). A prototype preservation page says an "X4" prototype is almost identical to retail 01, differing by extra "MIC1" holes and a missing "D9" diode, and that both 01 and 10 have D9 ([rvtr / RMC website, USG-CPU-X4](https://git.randommeaninglesscharacters.com/rvtr/rmc_website/src/commit/3e13e478523307549f8dde8a84a0fcdb597ec1a3/dsidev/release/USG-CPU-X4.html)) [community] (search excerpt only; site returned HTTP 503).
- Board **front** side (facing the user), as photographed by the FCC lab: the lower LCD module sits directly on the board centre; the D-pad, A/B/X/Y, Start and Select contact pads are printed on the board either side of it; LCD and touch ZIF connectors at the edges ([FCC Internal Photos 1, pages 3 to 4](https://fccid.io/BKEUSG-001/Internal-Photos/Internal-Photos-1-626991)) [observed].
- Board **back** side: Slot-1 card connector in a stamped steel cage (centre, opening to the back edge), long black Slot-2 connector along the front edge, Wi-Fi module under a shield can with a foam pad, 3.5 mm headphone jack at the front edge, volume slider, DC input connector at the back edge, and a region carrying only small passives and the Nintendo logo (where the battery sits) ([FCC Internal Photos 1, pages 2, 5, 6](https://fccid.io/BKEUSG-001/Internal-Photos/Internal-Photos-1-626991)) [observed].
- Wi-Fi module: FCC sample shows a module with a Mitsumi chip marked "MM3155" and a U.FL antenna connector ([FCC Internal Photos 2](https://fccid.io/BKEUSG-001/Internal-Photos/Internal-Photos-2-626992)) [observed]. A retail unit's back label reads "Contains FCC ID: EW4DWMW006 IC: 4250A-DWMW006" ([iFixit guide 4636 step 4 photo](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Lower+Case+Replacement/4636)) [observed]. The Wi-Fi board plugs into a board-to-board connector on the mainboard and is "secured to the motherboard with a layer of adhesive" ([iFixit Motherboard Replacement, guide 4784 steps 9 to 10](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Motherboard+Replacement/4784)) [measured: iFixit].
- Power switch: a slide switch on the right side of the bottom half, soldered to the mainboard with 6 solder joints and 2 plastic locating pegs, in the board's upper right corner directly behind the R button ([iFixit Power Button PCB, guide 136659](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Power+Button+PCB/136659)) [measured: iFixit]. Nintendo made it deliberately stiff so it would not switch in a bag (Sakiyama, [N.O.M interview](http://web.archive.org/web/20060405020918/http://www.nintendo.co.jp:80/nom/0604/42/index.html)) [official].
- Label electrical rating on a retail unit: "RATING: 5.2V 2.3W, BATTERY: Li-ion (USG-003) 3.7V", "5.2V IN" next to the DC jack ([iFixit guide 4636 step 4 photo](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Lower+Case+Replacement/4636)) [observed]. Max power consumption about 2.3 W while charging versus about 1.6 W for the original DS ([Game Watch, 2006-03-06](https://game.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/20060306/dsl.htm)) [official, reported].

**Displays**
- Lower LCD: 3 inch diagonal transmissive TFT, 256 x 192, 0.24 mm dot pitch, backlit with 4 brightness levels, with a resistive analogue touch panel ([Nintendo Japan spec page](https://www.nintendo.co.jp/ds/spec/index.html)) [official].
- FCC sample lower LCD label: "LS030B1DC48B"; upper LCD label: "LS030B1DC38B" ([FCC Internal Photos 3 page 1](https://fccid.io/BKEUSG-001/Internal-Photos/Internal-Photos-3-626993); [Internal Photos 1 page 1](https://fccid.io/BKEUSG-001/Internal-Photos/Internal-Photos-1-626991)) [observed]. The lower LCD has a silver metal frame and a short flex to the board.
- The touch panel is a separate layer: iFixit separates it from the LCD with a plastic opening tool run around the edges, and says a replacement panel may need the old "plastic black bezel" moved over ([iFixit Touchscreen Replacement, guide 4786 steps 18 to 20](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Touchscreen+Replacement/4786)) [measured: iFixit]. A repairer removes the digitizer by sliding a metal spudger "under the black sticker" in the front shell, implying it is held to the window by an adhesive frame (John Gotts, [iFixit guide 86279 step 27](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Disassembly/86279)) [community].
- When refitting the lower LCD, "ensure it has insulation on the back to prevent a short circuit with the motherboard" ([iFixit Lower LCD Replacement, guide 4785 steps 17, 20](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Lower+LCD+Replacement/4785)) [measured: iFixit].

**Battery (USG-003)**
- Model USG-003, Li-ion, "3.7V" ([iFixit guide 4636 step 4 photo of bay moulding and label](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Lower+Case+Replacement/4636)) [observed]; user manual names the rechargeable battery "Model No. USG-003" ([FCC User Manual 1](https://fccid.io/BKEUSG-001/User-Manual/User-Manual-1-627000)) [official].
- Capacity 1000 mAh, 3.7 Wh ([iFixit DS Lite replacement battery, part IF209-020-1](https://www.ifixit.com/products/nintendo-ds-lite-replacement-battery)) [vendor claim, iFixit; matches the label value commonly cited]. Nintendo engineer: the battery "looks the same size" as the DS one but has about 20% more electrical capacity (Yoneyama, [N.O.M interview](http://web.archive.org/web/20060405020918/http://www.nintendo.co.jp:80/nom/0604/42/index.html)) [official].
- Battery life (Nintendo): about 15 to 19 h at lowest brightness, 10 to 15 h low, 7 to 11 h high, 5 to 8 h highest; full charge about 3 h ([Nintendo Japan spec page](https://www.nintendo.co.jp/ds/spec/index.html)) [official].
- Captive Phillips screw in the battery cover; battery is pried out with a spudger and can be tight in the bay ([iFixit Battery Replacement, guide 4562](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Battery+Replacement/4562); [guide 86279 step 2](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Disassembly/86279)) [measured: iFixit; community].
- Aftermarket replacement dimensions (not the original): 48.8 x 33.4 x 6.9 mm, 850 mAh (Instrument Choice); 48.5 x 33 x 6 mm, 800 to 1000 mAh, 22 g (AccuCell via MediaMarkt); 48.71 x 36.33 x 6.24 mm, 850 mAh, "45 g" (Cameron Sino style listing on Newegg) (all search excerpt only: [Instrument Choice](https://www.instrumentchoice.com.au/products/gb-usg-003-electronic-game-battery-for-nintendo-ds-lite-amp-nds-2), [MediaMarkt](https://www.mediamarkt.at/de/product/_accucell-akku-passend-fur-nintendo-ds-lite-lipo-lithium-polymer-mp3-akku-1000-mah-108504742.html), [Newegg](https://www.newegg.com/p/14R-08VY-01WM0)) [vendor claim]. Subtel 900 mAh replacement: "Weight: 27 g, Length: 52 mm, Width: 35 mm, Height: 7 mm, 3.33Wh" ([Subtel](https://www.subtel.nl/en/Battery-for-Nintendo-DS-Lite-USG-001-USG-003-900mAh-Spare-Battery-Replacement-933066.html)) [vendor claim, fetched].

**Stylus**
- USG-004, about 87.5 mm long x 4.9 mm diameter (original DS: 75.0 mm x 4.0 mm) ([Nintendo Japan spec page](https://www.nintendo.co.jp/ds/spec/index.html); [Game Watch, 2006-03-06](https://game.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/20060306/dsl.htm)) [official].
- Inserted horizontally from the right side; the silo "just barely" fits, using the narrow space between the two cartridge slots and the battery as seen from the back (Sakiyama, [N.O.M interview](http://web.archive.org/web/20060405020918/http://www.nintendo.co.jp:80/nom/0604/42/index.html)) [official]. The silo is a separate plastic rail fixed inside the back shell with 3 gold screws ([iFixit guide 86279 step 25](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Disassembly/86279); [FCC Internal Photos 1 page 2](https://fccid.io/BKEUSG-001/Internal-Photos/Internal-Photos-1-626991)) [community; observed].

**Buttons**
- D-pad, A/B/X/Y and Start/Select are separate plastic caps over rubber membranes that lift straight out once the board is removed ([iFixit guide 86279 step 15](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Disassembly/86279); [iFixit Speaker Replacement, guide 1988 step 9](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Speaker+Replacement/1988)) [community; measured: iFixit].
- Button stroke is roughly double the original DS's, due to the rubber dome shape (there is a small gap before the dome contacts the board) ([Game Watch, 2006-03-06](https://game.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/20060306/dsl.htm)) [reviewer]. Nintendo tuned D-pad and face-button feel to be "close to the GameCube controller" (Yoneyama, [N.O.M interview](http://web.archive.org/web/20060405020918/http://www.nintendo.co.jp:80/nom/0604/42/index.html)) [official].
- Each shoulder button is three pieces: plastic button, metal pin, wire spring; "held in very loosely by small metal pegs and springs" and they fall out when the back shell is lifted ([iFixit guide 1922 steps 6 to 7](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Disassembling+Nintendo+DS+Lite+Bottom+Case/1922); [guide 4784 step 7](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Motherboard+Replacement/4784); [guide 122138](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Shoulder+Buttons+Replacement/122138)) [measured: iFixit]. They actuate "micro-button" switches on the board (guide 122138 step 4) [measured: iFixit].

**Top half contents (for completeness)**
- Speakers: two, in the top half, magnet type, soldered to the upper LCD flex ([iFixit guide 4812 steps 26, 28](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Speakers+Replacement/4812); [guide 1988 step 19](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Speaker+Replacement/1988); [FCC Internal Photos 1 page 1](https://fccid.io/BKEUSG-001/Internal-Photos/Internal-Photos-1-626991)) [measured: iFixit; observed]. Custom-made stereo speakers because stock parts would not fit the thin lid (Yoneyama, [N.O.M interview](http://web.archive.org/web/20060405020918/http://www.nintendo.co.jp:80/nom/0604/42/index.html)) [official].
- Microphone: moved to the centre of the hinge (for portrait play); a board-mounted mic would have been more space efficient (Yoneyama, [N.O.M interview](http://web.archive.org/web/20060405020918/http://www.nintendo.co.jp:80/nom/0604/42/index.html); [Game Watch, 2006-02-10](https://game.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/20060210/dslite.htm)) [official]. FCC photos show an electret capsule (with and without rubber boot) on a white wire ([FCC Internal Photos 3 pages 4 to 5](https://fccid.io/BKEUSG-001/Internal-Photos/Internal-Photos-3-626993)) [observed].
- Wi-Fi antenna: a small PCB antenna on a black coax, fixed in the top half beside the upper LCD ([FCC Internal Photos 3 pages 3, 6](https://fccid.io/BKEUSG-001/Internal-Photos/Internal-Photos-3-626993); [iFixit Wi-Fi Antenna Replacement, guide 4796](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Wi-Fi+Antenna+Replacement/4796)) [observed; measured: iFixit].
- Upper LCD: custom part, "stronger, yet thinner"; the lid "looks the same thickness as DS from outside but is quite thin seen from inside" (Sakiyama, [N.O.M interview](http://web.archive.org/web/20060405020918/http://www.nintendo.co.jp:80/nom/0604/42/index.html)) [official]. A repairer says the upper LCD is "protected by a plastic shield in front and a metal shield in back" ([iFixit guide 86279 step 21](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Disassembly/86279)) [community].

**Through the hinge**
- One upper-LCD flex cable plus the black antenna coax and the white mic wire. Both wires plug into the bottom half (antenna onto the Wi-Fi module's U.FL, mic onto the mainboard) and are routed through the centre of the flex and under the Slot-1 connector ([iFixit guide 3915 step 10](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Disconnecting+Nintendo+DS+Lite+Logic+Board/3915); [guide 4782 steps 1 to 2](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Motherboard+Replacement/4782); [guide 86279 steps 12, 13, 17, 18, 22](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Disassembly/86279)) [measured: iFixit; community].

### Inferences
- [inference] Everything a user grips (buttons, shoulder buttons, slots, battery, stylus) is in the bottom half; the top half is essentially a display, two small speakers and some wiring. For a single-slab device that wants to feel like "the bottom half", the relevant reference mass is the bottom half alone, not 218 g.
- [inference] The ">ABS,PC+ABS<" code is consistent with Sakiyama's statement that the outer faces are two-shot mouldings: one shot ABS, one shot PC+ABS (the clear or tinted cosmetic layer, or the structural layer). I did not find which layer is which.

### Gaps
- No public Nintendo service manual or bill of materials. iFixit's own short teardown points readers to "the service manual" but none is published.
- Exact retail board revisions and their differences are only known from forum posts (GBAtemp and the RMC page could not be fetched).
- No YouTube teardown video facts were recorded: searches returned no YouTube results, and I did not find a video with stated measurements.

## 2. Mass: total, halves, parts, centre of gravity and balance

### Takeaway
Nintendo's official mass is about 218 g including the built-in battery and stylus. Nobody I could find has published weighed values for the separate halves or for individual internal parts of a genuine DS Lite. The battery is plausibly about 20 to 25 g, the stylus about 1.5 to 2 g. By component reasoning the bottom half carries most of the mass (estimated 120 to 150 g, about 55 to 70% of 218 g, with large uncertainty), with the battery sitting behind the left grip. Nintendo's mechanical designers explicitly laid out parts for front-to-back and left-to-right balance in the hand.

### Cited Findings
- Mass about 218 g "including built-in rechargeable battery and stylus"; original DS about 275 g on the same basis ([Nintendo Japan spec page](https://www.nintendo.co.jp/ds/spec/index.html)) [official]. Press release, 2006-01-26: 218 g versus 275 g ([Nintendo press release, Wayback](http://web.archive.org/web/20070321172146/http://www.nintendo.co.jp:80/n10/news/060126.html)) [official].
- Size closed: 133.0 mm wide x 73.9 mm deep x 21.5 mm thick ([Nintendo Japan spec page](https://www.nintendo.co.jp/ds/spec/index.html)) [official]. Open height about 139 mm (5.47 in) ([dimensions.com](https://www.dimensions.com/element/nintendo-ds-lite)) [community, search excerpt only].
- Mass reduction versus DS: 57 g ([Game Watch, 2006-03-06](https://game.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/20060306/dsl.htm)) [official numbers]. [derived] 218 / 275 = 0.793, so 20.7% lighter.
- Balance as a design driver: "for a handheld we lay parts out while also considering the front/back and left/right centre-of-gravity balance when the user holds it" (Yoneyama, paraphrased translation, [N.O.M interview](http://web.archive.org/web/20060405020918/http://www.nintendo.co.jp:80/nom/0604/42/index.html); English version: "think about the total weight and how the machine balances from front to back and left to right when the user is holding it", [GameSpot, 2006-04-04, Wayback](http://web.archive.org/web/20070129031839/http://www.gamespot.com:80/news/6147089.html)) [official].
- Balance remark from users: a forum poster said the original DS has "a definate top heavy feeling, whereas I find the Lite to sit confortably" ([Penny Arcade forums, "ergonomics DS vs DS Lite"](https://forums.penny-arcade.com/discussion/10617/ergonomics-ds-vs-ds-lite)) [community] (search excerpt only; page now redirects).
- Reviewer on weight: the reduced size makes it "much lighter, which lets you play longer with less hand fatigue", but adults may get cramps from the small body ([Nintendo World Report review](https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/11365)) [reviewer]. Hands-on: the modest numeric reductions make "a big difference" in the hand ([Shacknews, 2006-03-27](https://www.shacknews.com/article/41445/nintendo-ds-lite-enamel-navy)) [reviewer].
- Battery mass: no measured weight of a genuine USG-003 found. Vendor figures for replacements spread from 20 g to 45 g: 20 g (a German retailer's 900 mAh listing seen only in a search excerpt; the [Kaufland OTB listing](https://www.kaufland.de/product/22858541/) is the likely page but could not be fetched), 22 g (AccuCell, search excerpt only), 27 g (Subtel, fetched), 37 g ([Galaxus](https://galaxus.it/en/s1/product/noname-battery-for-nintendo-dsl-ds-lite-replacement-battery-1-pcs-device-specific-batteries-37866730), search excerpt only), 45 g (Newegg, search excerpt only) [vendor claim]. The higher values likely include packaging.
- Battery position: in the retail back view (Slot-1 edge up, Slot-2 edge down), the battery bay is on the right, under the "5.2V IN" DC jack ([iFixit guide 4636 step 4 photo](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Lower+Case+Replacement/4636)) [observed]. The FCC board photo shows the component-free board region on that same side ([FCC Internal Photos 1 page 5](https://fccid.io/BKEUSG-001/Internal-Photos/Internal-Photos-1-626991)) [observed].

### Inferences
- [derived] Battery mass from energy: 3.7 Wh at a typical 2006 Li-ion pack energy density of 150 to 200 Wh/kg gives 3.7 / 0.20 = 18.5 g to 3.7 / 0.15 = 24.7 g. From volume: 48.5 x 33 x 6 mm = 9.6 cm³ at 2.0 to 2.5 g/cm³ gives 19.2 to 24.0 g. **[estimated] Genuine USG-003 is about 20 to 25 g**, which matches the 20 g and 22 g vendor figures. About 9 to 11% of the total 218 g.
- [derived] Stylus: a solid cylinder 87.5 mm x 4.9 mm diameter has volume pi x 2.45² x 87.5 = 1650 mm³ = 1.65 cm³; at about 1.05 g/cm³ (ABS) that is about 1.7 g. **[estimated] about 1.5 to 2 g.**
- [derived] Battery side: viewed from the back with the hinge up, right becomes the player's left when the unit is turned to face the player. So **the battery sits behind the left grip (D-pad side)**, at the left lateral end, spanning most of the front-to-back depth (iFixit back photo; FCC back-shell photo shows the bay as a boxed recess at one end). The DC jack is on the same side at the back edge. The stylus rail runs across the width at mid depth, between Slot-2 (front) and Slot-1 (back), and ends at the battery bay, which matches Sakiyama's description of the stylus living in the narrow space "between the two cartridge slots and the battery".
- [estimated] Half split, assumption-based, NOT measured: bottom half shells about 35 to 45 g (two walls of about 133 x 74 mm plus perimeter at about 1.2 to 1.5 mm, ABS/PC about 1.05 to 1.2 g/cm³), mainboard with Slot-1 cage, Slot-2 connector, Wi-Fi module and jack about 30 to 40 g, lower LCD plus touch panel about 15 to 25 g, battery 20 to 25 g, buttons, L/R, stylus, metal plate, screws about 8 to 12 g. Sum about 110 to 145 g. Top half (two bezels, glossy cover, metal LCD back shield, custom LCD, two magnet speakers, antenna, mic, hinge hardware) by difference 218 minus bottom gives about 75 to 105 g. **Working estimate: bottom half about 120 to 150 g (55 to 70% of 218 g), top half about 70 to 100 g.** Uncertainty is large (plus or minus 20 g); weigh a real unit before using this.
- [inference] Because the top half is a significant fraction (estimated 30 to 45%) of the mass and sits beyond the hinge when open, the open-and-held centre of gravity is pulled toward the hinge, i.e., the unit tends to tip "away" from the player, and the lid's mass acts as a lever on the hands. A bottom-half-only device will feel more front-heavy (toward the player) than an open DS Lite unless mass is placed toward the back edge.
- [inference] Within the bottom half, front-to-back mass looks roughly balanced: Slot-1 steel cage, DC jack and the back-shell metal plate along the back (hinge) edge; Slot-2 connector, GBA slot cover and headphone jack along the front edge; the battery spans the depth. Left-right, the battery (estimated 20 to 25 g) makes the left end heavier; the power switch, stylus entry and part of the board electronics on the right offset only part of it (positions from the photos above). I found no data on the net lateral offset.

### Gaps
- No published weights for: each half, the mainboard, either LCD, the touch panel, either shell, the GBA slot cover, the hinge hardware or the speakers. Recommended measurement (non-destructive): weigh the whole unit; then, with the unit open flat (about 180 degrees is not possible, so use a fixture at the playing angle), measure the centre of gravity by balancing on a knife edge in two directions; then remove only the battery (and stylus) and repeat. A destructive split (2 hinge screws) gives the half masses directly.
- No source states the open-and-held CoG location or explicitly says the DS Lite is front- or back-heavy.

## 3. Fasteners: count, type, length, location; clips and covers

### Takeaway
The back shell is held by 7 screws (3 silver tri-wing about 5.5 mm, 1 black tri-wing about 3.9 mm hidden in the Slot-1 opening, 2 gold Phillips about 4.3 mm under the two rubber feet, 1 silver Phillips about 3.3 mm in the battery bay) plus perimeter snap fits, and on some units adhesive. The mainboard is screwed to the front shell with 2 small Phillips screws (about 3.4 mm), one of them through the battery bay. Inside the back shell, the stylus rail (3 screws) and a metal plate (2 screws) are separately screwed. The top half is held into the front shell by 2 long Phillips screws (about 8.3 mm) at one hinge end. The battery cover uses 1 captive Phillips screw.

### Cited Findings
- Back shell screws, iFixit (lengths measured to 0.1 mm): "Two 4.3 mm gold Phillips screws; One 3.9 mm black Tri-point screw; One 3.3 mm silver Phillips screw; Three 5.5 mm silver Tri-point screws" ([iFixit guide 4636 step 4](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Lower+Case+Replacement/4636)) [measured: iFixit]. The count of 2 board screws comes from iFixit; Gotts' sequence (two silver in the battery bay plus a "final screw holding down the motherboard", [guide 86279 steps 4, 9](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Disassembly/86279)) is consistent with one board screw being reached through the battery bay.
- [inference] Screw spacing: 7 back-shell screws on a 133 x 74 mm footprint, with 4 near corners and 3 along the back (Slot-1) edge, plus the battery-bay screw near the centre of one side. The back (hinge) edge, which carries the hinge loads and the L/R buttons, gets the densest fastening.

### Gaps
- Thread size (M1.4 or M1.7 class, self-tapping or machine) not published. Not found: screw head diameters.
- Number and location of snap hooks around the perimeter not documented in text; they would need a physical shell or clearer photos.

## 4. Stack-up in section through the bottom half

### Takeaway
Front to back: front shell (matte inner face with LCD window; the glossy two-shot face is on the back shell) / touch panel adhered behind the window with a black adhesive frame / lower LCD module in a metal frame, insulated on its back / 6-layer mainboard (button pads on its front face, rubber membranes between board and front shell) / tall parts on the board's back (Slot-1 steel cage, Slot-2 connector, shielded Wi-Fi module, headphone jack) / back shell, with the battery in its own bay beside the slots and the stylus rail between slots and battery. No layer thicknesses or board thickness have been published.

### Cited Findings
- Board front carries the lower LCD directly; the LCD needs insulation against the board ([FCC Internal Photos 1 page 3](https://fccid.io/BKEUSG-001/Internal-Photos/Internal-Photos-1-626991) [observed]; [iFixit guide 4785](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Lower+LCD+Replacement/4785) [measured: iFixit]).
- Touch panel separate from LCD, with a black plastic bezel; front-shell window adhesion via black sticker ([guide 4786](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Touchscreen+Replacement/4786); [guide 86279 step 27](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Disassembly/86279)) [measured: iFixit; community].
- With the board removed, the lower LCD and touch panel stay seated in the front shell window ([FCC Internal Photos 3 page 2](https://fccid.io/BKEUSG-001/Internal-Photos/Internal-Photos-3-626993)) [observed].
- When the board is lifted, "the buttons may fall out" (caps and membranes sit loose between board and front shell) ([guide 1988 step 9](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Speaker+Replacement/1988)) [measured: iFixit].
- Battery: in a bay in the back shell, beside the slots; stylus silo between the cartridge slots and the battery (Sakiyama, [N.O.M interview](http://web.archive.org/web/20060405020918/http://www.nintendo.co.jp:80/nom/0604/42/index.html)) [official].
- Board layers: "It's 6 I sanded it down slowly" (KreativDax, 2019-06-26), described by a reply as one power plane, one ground plane and four trace layers ([BitBuilt "DS lite pcb" thread](https://bitbuilt.net/forums/threads/ds-lite-pcb.2777/)) [community]. Board thickness: not stated there or anywhere I found.
- GBA cartridges protrude about 1 cm from Slot-2 because the slot was made shallow ([Game Watch, 2006-02-10](https://game.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/20060210/dslite.htm); [Game Watch, 2006-03-06](https://game.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/20060306/dsl.htm)) [official, reported].
- Closed thickness of both halves together: 21.5 mm ([Nintendo Japan spec page](https://www.nintendo.co.jp/ds/spec/index.html)) [official]. Touchscreen has a raised bezel so the two screens do not touch when closed ([Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/41445/nintendo-ds-lite-enamel-navy)) [reviewer].

### Inferences
- [inference] Because the battery sits in its own bay beside the board's slot area rather than under the board, the bottom half's thickness is set by the board plus the Slot-2 connector and GBA cartridge envelope, not by battery on board. This is the same "battery beside, not behind" trick a thin design needs.
- [inference] The LCD bearing directly on the board (with insulation) and the board screwed to the front shell means the front shell, LCD and board act as one sandwich; the back shell then closes over the connectors.

### Gaps
- No published thicknesses for: front shell wall, touch panel, lower LCD module, board, Slot-2 connector height, back shell wall, battery bay floor, or the split of 21.5 mm between the two halves. Measure from a physical unit (calipers on each half; board edge with micrometer).

## 5. Structural features: ribs, bosses, metal, foam, adhesive

### Takeaway
There is no metal chassis or full shield in the bottom half. Stiffness comes from the two plastic shells (with internal ribs, a grid-ribbed area and many screw bosses), the screwed-in stylus rail acting as a cross beam, the board clamped to the front shell, one small stamped steel plate in the back shell, and metal in the components themselves (Slot-1 cage, Wi-Fi shield can, LCD frame). Nintendo says it reinforced the internal structure and adjusted component placement for strength, and ran long strength tests.

### Cited Findings
- Nintendo: "we raised strength considerably, including reinforcing the internal structure and balancing the placement of parts, and spent a long time on strength testing; but there is a conflict with weight and size" (Yoneyama, paraphrased translation, [N.O.M interview](http://web.archive.org/web/20060405020918/http://www.nintendo.co.jp:80/nom/0604/42/index.html); English: "we've beefed up the internal structure and layout, and we've spent a lot of time doing strength testing", [GameSpot, Wayback](http://web.archive.org/web/20070129031839/http://www.gamespot.com:80/news/6147089.html)) [official].
- Back shell interior, FCC sample: a long plastic stylus rail running parallel to the hinge across most of the width, fixed with 3 screws; a stamped sheet-metal plate (roughly half the width long, narrow) with a grey foam pad, fixed with 2 screws on the Slot-1/back-edge side; a grid of shallow ribs; multiple cylindrical screw bosses at the corners and along the back edge; the battery bay as a boxed recess with a through-hole for the board screw ([FCC Internal Photos 1 page 2](https://fccid.io/BKEUSG-001/Internal-Photos/Internal-Photos-1-626991)) [observed]. Gotts calls the plate the "metal shield" ([guide 86279 step 25](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Disassembly/86279)) [community].
- Front shell interior, FCC sample: button wells, ribs along the hinge edge, corner bosses, LCD window ([FCC Internal Photos 3 page 2](https://fccid.io/BKEUSG-001/Internal-Photos/Internal-Photos-3-626993)) [observed].
- Foam: a foam pad on top of the Wi-Fi shield can ([FCC Internal Photos 2 page 2](https://fccid.io/BKEUSG-001/Internal-Photos/Internal-Photos-2-626992)) [observed]; foam on the metal plate (above) [observed]; a pink foam or tape strip beside the upper LCD in the top half ([FCC Internal Photos 1 page 1](https://fccid.io/BKEUSG-001/Internal-Photos/Internal-Photos-1-626991)) [observed].
- Adhesive: Wi-Fi board to mainboard ("the adhesive is very strong", [guide 86279 step 7](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Disassembly/86279); [guide 4784 step 10](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Motherboard+Replacement/4784)); some back shells adhesive-sealed ([guide 4636 step 6](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Lower+Case+Replacement/4636)); touch panel black adhesive frame ([guide 86279 step 27](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Disassembly/86279)) [measured: iFixit; community].
- Top half: rear display bezel slides about 2 mm to release from the front display bezel ([guide 4812 step 24](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Speakers+Replacement/4812)) [measured: iFixit]; the outer plastic cover is "wedged underneath the back metal cover" and held by tabs, with schemes varying by production age ([guide 86279 step 31](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Disassembly/86279)) [community].
- Build feel, reviewers: "put together just as tightly as other Nintendo handhelds. It feels sturdy and durable" (Construction score 9) ([Nintendo World Report review](https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/11365)) [reviewer]; "All lines are clean and concise", no gaps when folded ([Shacknews, 2006-03-27](https://www.shacknews.com/article/41445/nintendo-ds-lite-enamel-navy)) [reviewer].

### Inferences
- [derived] Bulk density (official mass / official closed bounding box): DS Lite 218 g / (133.0 x 73.9 x 21.5 mm = 211.3 cm³) = **1.03 g/cm³**, versus original DS 275 g / 364.0 cm³ = 0.76 g/cm³ and GBA SP 143 g / 168.6 cm³ = 0.85 g/cm³ (see section 8 for sources). The DS Lite is about 36% denser than the original DS (1.03 / 0.76 = 1.36). [inference] This "about as dense as water or solid ABS" figure is probably the single biggest numeric reason it feels solid rather than hollow; a printed case with a lot of empty volume will feel lighter than its gram count unless it is similarly packed.
- [inference] "Absence of creak" plausibly comes from: board screwed to the front shell and the LCD bearing on it (no floating stack), 7 back-shell screws including 3 along the back edge, the stylus rail screwed in as a beam, adhesive on some units, and short unsupported spans (most panel areas are backed by the battery, connectors or ribs). None of these were individually tested in a source.
- [inference] The single steel plate is too small to add meaningful mass (an estimated 2 to 4 g) or global stiffness; its foam pad suggests it presses on and grounds or retains something (Slot-1 cage or Wi-Fi area). Its function is undocumented.

### Gaps
- No wall thickness, rib height or material grade data. No published torsion or drop test data. Purpose of the back-shell metal plate undocumented.

## 6. Hinge: construction, attachment, detent, known crack

### Takeaway
The hinge is plastic knuckles on both halves with metal hinge parts inside: a round metal hinge shaft that stays with the top half, and a metal clicker unit with a ring inside a knuckle that gives two detent stops (fully open, and one stop before it). The top half is clamped into the bottom half's front shell by 2 long (about 8.3 mm) Phillips screws at one hinge end; with those removed, the halves slide apart sideways. The upper LCD flex wraps around a metal cylinder in the hinge and is the fragile part. The famous crack is a hairline in the plastic on the left side of the hinge, reported from mid 2006; Nintendo called it cosmetic and below 0.02% of US units, then repaired it free.

### Cited Findings
- Attachment: "Remove the two Phillips-head screws that hold the hinge in place. Open the system up ... carefully unhinge them from each other by sliding the bottom piece to the left and the top piece to the right" ([iFixit guide 1988 steps 12 to 13](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Speaker+Replacement/1988)) [measured: iFixit]. Screws are 8.3 mm Phillips ([guide 4812 step 17](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Speakers+Replacement/4812)) [measured: iFixit].
- Shaft: "remove the round hinge shaft. This should be in the top screen piece" ([guide 1988 step 14](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Speaker+Replacement/1988)) [measured: iFixit].
- Metal hinge internals: Gotts' "latch" step shows a metal cylindrical part with a ring seated in a white plastic hinge knuckle, and the 2 screws at the front-shell hinge corner; he calls the assembly "the chief design flaw" and says to "save the screws and the metal and plastic piece" when changing shells ([guide 86279 step 16](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Disassembly/86279)) [community; observed]. Aftermarket hinge kits contain "2x Hinges, 2x Rings"; a buyer found the aftermarket metal ring longer than factory, so the top did not sit flush, and reused the original ring ([Hand Held Legend DS Lite hinge](https://handheldlegend.com/en-fr/products/nintendo-ds-lite-hinge-replcement)) [vendor claim; community].
- Detent: the hinge stops at two positions, fully open and one stage before it, like the original DS ([Game Watch, 2006-03-06](https://game.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/20060306/dsl.htm)) [reviewer].
- Hinge spring: a Reddit thread says the hinge spring often seizes, that a seized spring can crack the shell, that white springs are hard to source, and that black aftermarket springs have "less snap" ([r/NintendoDS "How could I fix this"](https://www.reddit.com/r/NintendoDS/comments/1f0bfm8/how_could_i_fix_this/)) [community] (search excerpt only; Reddit not fetchable).
- Flex routing: the upper LCD flex is threaded through a slit in the case and curled through the display hinge; Gotts' photo shows the flex wrapped around a metal cylinder in a white plastic holder; "The flex cable is incredibly fragile, and it was strengthened in later models" ([guide 4812 step 18](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Speakers+Replacement/4812); [guide 4800 step 29](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Upper+LCD+Replacement/4800); [guide 86279 steps 18, 24](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Disassembly/86279)) [measured: iFixit; community; observed]. Routing the white mic wire wrongly is "the most common reason why DS Lites don't go together properly" ([guide 86279 step 17](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Disassembly/86279)) [community].
- Crack location: "the left hinge cracking" in photos from four owners, at least eight confirmations, $50 out-of-warranty repair, 2006-07-17 ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2006-07-17-nintendo-ds-lite-cracks-on-hinge.html)) [reviewer/community]. Joystiq's own unit: crack on the left side of the hinge, not dropped or sat on, opened and closed daily and carried in pockets and bags ([Engadget/Joystiq, 2006-07-17](https://www.engadget.com/2006-07-17-behold-joystiqs-cracked-ds-lite.html)) [reviewer].
- Nintendo of America: "the reported number of small cosmetic cracks in the plastic hinge of DS Lite systems" is "less than 0.02 percent of the total units sold" in the US; "This cosmetic issue in no way impacts the gameplay or integrity of the DS Lite" (2006-07-21) ([Game Developer / Gamasutra](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/pc/nintendo-agrees-to-fix-ds-lite-hinge-cracks)) [official]. Earlier statement: "We are not aware of any problems with the hinge of the DS Lite" (2006-07-18) ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2006-07-18-nintendo-we-are-not-aware-of-any-problems-with-the-hinge-of-th.html)) [official]. NOA then offered free repair to callers ([Engadget/AOL, 2006-07-21](https://www.aol.com/news/2006-07-21-nintendo-fesses-up-to-ds-lite-hinge-defect-will-fix-for-free.html)) [reviewer] (search excerpt only).
- The top-half lid is the part that "tends to get bumped", which is why Nintendo specified a stronger, thinner custom upper LCD ([N.O.M interview](http://web.archive.org/web/20060405020918/http://www.nintendo.co.jp:80/nom/0604/42/index.html); [GameSpot, Wayback](http://web.archive.org/web/20070129031839/http://www.gamespot.com:80/news/6147089.html)) [official].

### Inferences
- [inference] The 2 hinge screws are at the right hinge end (they sit beside the LED windows, and Nintendo places the LEDs to the right of the hinge). The crack reports are on the left end. I found no source linking these, so do not treat it as a cause.
- [inference] For the new design: a lid clamped by only 2 screws at one end, with a plastic knuckle carrying the detent load, is the known weak point; metal-to-plastic detent loads at a thin knuckle are what cracked. A single-slab handheld avoids this entirely; if a lid is kept, put the detent load into a metal part screwed into bosses, not into a thin knuckle wall.

### Gaps
- No exploded drawing of the hinge clicker (cam profile, spring type, torque). No official root cause for the crack; no statement of which shell (top or bottom) the crack is in, though most photos referenced are of the hinge on the lid side.
- Opening torque and detent force not measured anywhere I found.

## 7. Teardown sequence and assembly tolerances

### Takeaway
Order: battery cover, battery, rubber feet, 7 back-shell screws, Slot-2 cover, pry back shell around the perimeter, catch the L/R buttons, peel the adhesive Wi-Fi board, unlatch the touch flex, remove 2 board screws, flip the board to unlatch the upper and lower LCD flexes and unplug and thread out the antenna and mic wires, lift out the board, then the button caps and membranes and the lower LCD and touch panel. Tolerance-critical points are wire routing through the hinge, switch cap alignment, L/R spring seating, and hinge ring length for flush closing.

### Cited Findings
- Step outline with sources:
  1. Battery cover (1 captive Phillips), battery out ([iFixit guide 4562](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Battery+Replacement/4562)).
  2. Pry off 2 rubber feet, remove 7 screws, pull the Slot-2 cover ([guide 4636 steps 3 to 5](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Lower+Case+Replacement/4636)).
  3. Spudger around the perimeter, lift the back shell keeping the unit flat so the L/R buttons and springs stay put ([guide 1922 steps 6 to 7](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Disassembling+Nintendo+DS+Lite+Bottom+Case/1922)).
  4. Remove L/R buttons (pin and spring), then the Wi-Fi board (adhesive) "or you risk damaging the digitizer" ([guide 86279 steps 6 to 7](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Disassembly/86279); [guide 4784 steps 7 to 10](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Motherboard+Replacement/4784)).
  5. Flip the touch-panel ZIF latch, pull the flex ([guide 3915 step 9](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Disconnecting+Nintendo+DS+Lite+Logic+Board/3915)).
  6. Remove the 2 board screws (3.4 mm), lift the board's front edge, open the upper LCD ZIF and release it ([guide 4782 steps 4 to 6](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Motherboard+Replacement/4782)).
  7. Unplug the white mic wire and black antenna wire and thread them out under the Slot-1 connector ([guide 4782 steps 1 to 2](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Motherboard+Replacement/4782); [guide 86279 steps 12 to 13](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Disassembly/86279)).
  8. Release the lower LCD flex; lift out button caps and membranes; separate touch panel from LCD ([guide 4785](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Lower+LCD+Replacement/4785); [guide 4786](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Touchscreen+Replacement/4786)).
  9. Top half: 2 x 8.3 mm hinge screws, pass the upper flex down through the slit and the wires through the hole, remove 4 bezel screw covers and 4 x 3.4 mm screws, slide the rear bezel about 2 mm, separate ([guide 4812 steps 17 to 25](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Speakers+Replacement/4812)).
  [measured: iFixit; community for 86279]
- Other guides: full community disassembly ([iFixit guide 86279, John Gotts](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Disassembly/86279)); iFixit teardowns ([42650](https://www.ifixit.com/Teardown/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Teardown/42650), [73109](https://www.ifixit.com/Teardown/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Teardown/73109)) are thin; the iFixit DS Lite device page lists 36 guides ([iFixit Nintendo DS Lite](https://www.ifixit.com/Device/Nintendo_DS_Lite)).
- Tolerance and fit notes:
  - Power and volume switch caps must line up with the board switches ([guide 1922 step 7](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Disassembling+Nintendo+DS+Lite+Bottom+Case/1922)) [measured: iFixit].
  - White mic wire routing decides whether the shells close properly; if routed right "the finished product will feel like it is fresh off the factory floor" ([guide 86279 step 17](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Disassembly/86279)) [community].
  - Antenna cable can kink when re-fed under the Slot-1 connector; iFixit suggests pulling it through with thread ([guide 4782 step 2](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Motherboard+Replacement/4782)) [measured: iFixit].
  - Touch-panel ZIF flap breaks if opened past 90 degrees ([guide 4782 step 3](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Motherboard+Replacement/4782)) [measured: iFixit].
  - Aftermarket hinge ring too long means the lid does not sit flush ([Hand Held Legend reviews](https://handheldlegend.com/en-fr/products/nintendo-ds-lite-hinge-replcement)) [community].
  - Factory gaps: "no gaps when folded" ([Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/41445/nintendo-ds-lite-enamel-navy)) [reviewer]; a reviewer noted the matte inside is "off-tone" and the fold edges look a lighter colour ([Nintendo World Report](https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/11365)) [reviewer].
  - DS card eject: pushes out about 1 cm and stops if the finger is released, so a spring-ejected card cannot fly out (Yoneyama, [N.O.M interview](http://web.archive.org/web/20060405020918/http://www.nintendo.co.jp:80/nom/0604/42/index.html)) [official].

### Inferences
- [inference] The design depends on loose parts (L/R pins and springs, button caps, membranes) being trapped by the shells, so shell-to-shell registration and boss height control button preload and rattle. Any printed case must hold these with the same or tighter tolerance.

## 8. Context: original DS, DSi, 3DS, GBA SP; how the Lite got smaller and lighter

### Takeaway
The DS Lite cut 15.7 mm of width, 10.8 mm of depth, 7.4 mm of thickness (42% of box volume) but only 57 g (21%) from the original DS, giving the highest bulk density of its era until the DSi and 3DS. Nintendo did it with custom parts (stronger, thinner upper LCD; custom thin speakers), a higher-capacity battery of the same size, a shallower GBA slot (carts stick out 1 cm), a stylus moved to a sideways silo between the slots and battery, the mic moved to the hinge, the power button replaced by a side slide switch, and LEDs moved to the hinge.

- Original DS LCDs were transflective; DS Lite's are transmissive with 4 brightness levels ([Nintendo Japan spec page](https://www.nintendo.co.jp/ds/spec/index.html)) [official].
- Nintendo engineers: size cut without changing screen size or button layout, by co-developing custom parts with suppliers; upper LCD custom, stronger and thinner; stereo speakers custom for thinness; battery same apparent size with about 20% more capacity ([N.O.M interview](http://web.archive.org/web/20060405020918/http://www.nintendo.co.jp:80/nom/0604/42/index.html)) [official].
- Stylus storage moved to the right side, horizontal, because the smaller body no longer had room for a vertical silo; stylus 1 cm longer and 1 mm thicker ([Game Watch, 2006-02-10](https://game.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/20060210/dslite.htm); [N.O.M interview](http://web.archive.org/web/20060405020918/http://www.nintendo.co.jp:80/nom/0604/42/index.html)) [official].

- GBA slot shallower, carts protrude about 1 cm ([Game Watch, 2006-02-10](https://game.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/20060210/dslite.htm)) [official, reported].
- Iwata Asks (DSi): "The LCD unit's weight is proportional to its area", yet the DSi with a 3.25 inch screen (DS Lite 3.0 inch) "ended up being a little lighter than the DS Lite"; the DSi moved to a matte texture because fingerprints showed on black DS Lites; an early two-slot DSi mock-up "was about three millimeters thicker" and felt too big ([Iwata Asks: Nintendo DSi, Vol. 1, pages 2 to 3](https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/ds/dsi/0/1/), [page 3](https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/ds/dsi/0/2/)) [official].
- Iwata: "The DS Lite possessed a lot of improvements over the original DS, but one thing that was sacrificed was the sound" ([Iwata Asks: Nintendo DSi, Vol. 1 page 3](https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/ds/dsi/0/2/)) [official]. Game Watch heard less bass and lower maximum volume from the DS Lite speakers than the DS ([Game Watch, 2006-03-06](https://game.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/20060306/dsl.htm)) [reviewer].
- iFixit's DSi teardown compares the DSi with the DS Lite side by side and credits the DSi's rougher matte shell with "better grip" ([iFixit DSi teardown](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Headphone+Plug+Replacement/714)) [reviewer] (search excerpt only).

### Inferences
- [derived] DS to DS Lite: width 148.7 to 133.0 mm is minus 15.7 mm (minus 10.6%); depth 84.7 to 73.9 mm is minus 10.8 mm (minus 12.8%); thickness 28.9 to 21.5 mm is minus 7.4 mm (minus 25.6%); box volume 364.0 to 211.3 cm³ is minus 42%; mass minus 20.7%.
- [derived] Bulk density (mass / closed box volume): DS 0.76, GBA SP 0.85, DSi XL 1.01, DS Lite 1.03, DSi 1.10, 3DS 1.13 g/cm³. Areal density (mass / footprint): DS Lite 218 g / 98.3 cm² = 2.22 g/cm²; DS 2.18; DSi 2.09; 3DS 2.37; GBA SP 2.06 g/cm². So the Lite did not get lighter per unit of footprint; it got much thinner while keeping almost the same mass per cm² of hand contact. [inference] That is the physical signature of "small but dense".
- [derived] DSi versus DS Lite thickness: 21.5 minus 18.9 = 2.6 mm, 2.6 / 21.5 = 12.1% thinner, for 4 g less mass.
- [inference] Iwata's "LCD weight is proportional to area" gives a scaling rule for the new 3.5 inch panel: at the same 4:3 aspect, area scales with diagonal squared, (3.5 / 3.0)² = 1.36, so a comparable-technology panel would weigh about 36% more than one DS Lite LCD. A 3.5 inch IPS module of a different construction (thicker backlight, cover glass) may differ; weigh the actual part.
- [inference] For the new handheld (3.5 inch IPS, LiPo, speaker, printed two-part case): to match the DS Lite's in-hand density, aim for roughly 1.0 g/cm³ of closed envelope, and roughly 2.2 g/cm² over the gripped footprint if the device is a single slab of similar thickness. Printed PLA/PETG at partial infill will make this harder; mass may need to come from a denser shell (100% infill walls), the cell placed toward the back edge, or a steel plate.
