# DS Lite (USG-001) bottom-half controls: construction and tactile feel

Scope: the Nintendo DS Lite only. Original DS ("phat", NTR-001), DSi and DSi XL appear only as labelled comparisons. Builds on `hardware/ref_design/case/DSLITE.md` and `hardware/ref_design/board/membrane/README.md`; where this file agrees with them it says so in a word, and spends its space on what is new or contradicts them.

Confidence labels: **official** (a Nintendo document or a manufacturer datasheet); **measured** (someone measured a part, named); **derived** (computed by this researcher from a named open file or photo, method stated); **estimated** (derived with a large error bar, usually from a photo); **press** (a journalist's hands-on description, qualitative); **community claim** (forum, blog, vendor or project text, not checked). "Search excerpt" marks pages that blocked direct fetching (GBAtemp, eBay, some others), where only the search engine's extract was seen.

Files downloaded for measurement sit under `/tmp/claude-1000/-home-alex-Code-mega-blastoise/5d435370-92de-4303-8bf6-69f783ed9bab/scratchpad/dslite/controls/` (the archived Nintendo manual PDF, the Pi Tin STL zip and schematic, Hand Held Legend product photos, two Wikimedia Commons photos). Nothing from them was copied into the repo.

## 1. D-pad: cap shape, pivot, membrane, diagonals and PCB contacts

### Takeaway
The pivot question is answered: the genuine DS Lite D-pad has a **peg on the underside of the cap that passes through a hole in the centre of a one-piece, four-dome silicone pad** and bears on the board, and the press reported the centre bump as clearly felt in use. The top is a **shallow dish that rises toward the arm tips** (the original DS had a round central dimple instead). Contact pitch 14.0 mm is now corroborated by a second shell built for genuine caps. No measured travel or force exists.

### Cited Findings
- Game Watch hands-on (Kenji Saeki, 2006-03-06), translated: the original DS D-pad "has a round dip in the centre, while the DS Lite only curves gently toward the centre" (「DSのほうは中央部が丸くへこんでいるが、DS Liteは緩やかに中央に向かってアールがかかっている程度」), and "pressing it makes it clear there is a protrusion at the centre of the D-pad" (「十字ボタン中央部に突起があることも押してみればはっきりわかる」). The D-pad shows the same longer-press difference as the face buttons, and is smaller than the DS's. ([Game Watch](https://game.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/20060306/dsl.htm); press)
- Ars Technica review (Ben Kuchera, 2006-06-11): "The D-pad is a touch smaller than what you got with the original DS, and it's a bit less satisfying in the click department." ([Ars Technica p.4, archived](https://web.archive.org/web/2006/http://arstechnica.com/reviews/hardware/dslite.ars/4); summarised by [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2006-06-12-the-ds-lite-is-smaller-than-a-brick.html); press)
- Nintendo World Report hands-on (Josh Daugherty, 2006-03-12): "The new and improved D-pad is a little more elevated than that of the original DS", "very responsive to the slightest touch". ([NWR](https://nintendoworldreport.com/hands-on-preview/11190); press)
- Pi Tin build guide, genuine DS Lite caps: "fit the D-pad membrane over the peg at the center of the D-pad". For its own printed D-pad the TPU membrane instead carries a rounded central nub that is "intentionally oversized" and "designed to compress slightly"; trim it with flush cutters if the D-pad is hard to press. ([Pi Tin hardware_build.md](https://github.com/jackw01/pi-tin/blob/main/hardware_build.md); community claim, CERN-OHL-W project)
- Pi Tin's render of the back of its front panel with genuine-cap models shows the cap's rounded central peg standing through the black membrane, with the membrane's four nubs on the arms. ([front_panel_dslite_buttons.png](https://github.com/jackw01/pi-tin/blob/main/images/front_panel_dslite_buttons.png); community claim, CAD render)
- Pi Tin STL `p014 ds lite dpad membrane TPU` (made to sit under a genuine D-pad cap): a 17.80 mm disc, 1.35 mm overall, with a **2.50 mm centre hole** (radius 1.25) and four nubs spanning radius 6.25 to 7.75 mm, i.e. 1.5 mm wide nubs centred on **r = 7.0 mm, a 14.0 mm opposite-direction pitch**. ([pi_tin_stl.zip](https://github.com/jackw01/pi-tin/blob/main/3d/pi_tin_stl.zip); derived by planar sections of the STL)
- Pi Tin STL `p004 d pad` (its printed stand-in for the genuine cap): 17.80 x 17.80 mm cross, 3.34 mm tall, flange 0.50 mm thick reaching r 6.25 to 6.55 mm between the arms. Top surface height along an arm: 2.85 mm at the centre, 2.96 at r 4, 3.10 at r 6, 3.28 at r 8.0, then a rounded tip (3.16 at r 8.7, 2.95 at r 8.85). That is a **0.43 mm rise from centre to r 8 mm, close to a concave sphere of about 74 mm radius**, with roughly a 0.5 mm round on the arm ends. The printed cap has no peg (its pivot is the membrane nub). ([pi_tin_stl.zip](https://github.com/jackw01/pi-tin/blob/main/3d/pi_tin_stl.zip); derived by ray-sampling the top surface; it is a replica, not a scan)
- Aftermarket D-pad membrane (Hand Held Legend product photos): one four-lobed piece, four round domes at the corners of a square, carbon pills on the underside, and a **round through-hole at the centre** (about 3 mm across, estimated from the photo). ([HHL DS Lite Silicone Membranes](https://handheldlegend.com/products/nintendo-ds-lite-silicone-button-pads); estimated)
- In the same photo, D-pad dome spacing is 0.964 of the ABXY pad's (80.5 px vs 83.5 px), which would make the D-pad pitch about 14.5 mm if ABXY is 15.0. Inconclusive between 14.0 and 15.0 because of lens perspective. (HHL photo NDL1471 on the [same page](https://handheldlegend.com/products/nintendo-ds-lite-silicone-button-pads); estimated)
- Board revisions differ here: gorgyrip (BitBuilt, 2024-12-23) found USG-CPU-01 "99.99% identical to USG-CPU-10. Differences: dpad footprint and 3 gnd vias." ([BitBuilt thread](https://bitbuilt.net/forums/posts/65656/); community claim)
- The RMC write-up of the X4 prototype DS Lite board says the retail CPU-10 has a D9 diode on the D-pad connectors that the prototype lacks. ([RMC USG-CPU-X4](https://git.randommeaninglesscharacters.com/rvtr/rmc_website/src/commit/3e13e478523307549f8dde8a84a0fcdb597ec1a3/dsidev/release/USG-CPU-X4.html); search excerpt, community claim)
- DS Lite buttons are active low: "The way the DS Lite buttons work is by pulling them to ground"; the D-pad signals are on test points TP1, TP4, TP3, TP2 and TP6. ([facelesstech blog, 2022-07-23](https://facelesstech.wordpress.com/2022/07/23/ds-lite-clicky-buttons/); community claim)
- Diagonals: an iFixit asker (2025-12-14) had all four directions working but diagonals "spotty"; cleaning and one replacement membrane did not help, a third membrane did ("the replacement membrane I tried was also worn out"). ([iFixit Q&A 930769](https://www.ifixit.com/Answers/View/930769/D-pad+diagonal+inputs+don't+register+correctly); community claim)
- Conflicting claim: an OSHPark board "Ds lite Boardies" (24.2 x 24.2 mm, 2 layers) says it is a "Four button board for abxy, or dpad. uses the same dimensions as ds lite buttons, so rubber pads work 1:1", implying one pitch for both clusters. ([OSHPark](https://oshpark.com/shared_projects/wgKz8oJF); community claim, contradicts the 14.0 / 15.0 split)

### Inferences
- Pivot: three independent sources (Pi Tin's text and render, the centre hole in every aftermarket D-pad pad, and Game Watch's felt centre bump) agree that the rocking point is a **peg on the cap through the membrane onto the board**. The membrane is not the pivot. Our PCB therefore needs a flat, hard, bare landing spot at the D-pad centre: no via, component, silkscreen or mask edge under the peg, and the peg's length relative to the dome height sets how far each arm can travel before bottoming.
- Pi Tin's 2.50 mm hole fits over the genuine peg, so the peg is at most about 2.5 mm across (TPU can grip a slightly larger peg); the genuine pad's hole is about 3 mm, giving the peg clearance to rock.
- Diagonal geometry: rocking about a centre peg, a diagonal press tilts about an axis through the peg at 45 degrees to the arms, so each of the two loaded domes (at r 7.0 mm) sits only 7.0 x cos 45 = 4.95 mm from the tilt axis and compresses about 0.71 of what a cardinal press gives at the same tilt. Diagonals therefore need more tilt and are the first input to fail when domes are worn, too tall or too stiff, which fits the iFixit report. A replica membrane that is even slightly taller than genuine will lose diagonals first.
- Two pitches (14.0 D-pad from Ampersand's gerbers and Pi Tin's nubs, 15.0 ABXY) remain the best working values; the OSHPark "same dimensions" note probably reflects a board that tolerates the 1 mm difference rather than a measured identity.

### Gaps
- Genuine peg diameter and length, cap proud height above the face, travel per direction and tilt angle at contact: no measurement or drawing found.
- Whether the genuine top is spherical, conical or flat-arm-with-dimple: only Game Watch's words and Pi Tin's replica. Edge chamfers on the genuine arms: unknown.
- D-pad carbon pill diameter and dome height: not published anywhere found.
- The USG-CPU-01 versus -10 D-pad footprint difference is undocumented beyond gorgyrip's one line; his scans are in a 1.35 GB Dropbox folder linked from the BitBuilt thread (not downloaded).

## 2. A/B/X/Y: caps, legends, travel and click versus the original DS and DSi

### Takeaway
The DS Lite's face buttons are **hard caps on carbon-pill silicone domes that close directly on the board**, while the original DS, DSi and GBA SP put **metal-dome tact switches under the rubber**. That is the whole explanation of the "DS clicks, DS Lite presses" difference: a Japanese launch hands-on measured it by feel as **about double the stroke**. No grams-force number exists for any DS Lite button.

### Cited Findings
- Game Watch (2006-03-06), translated: "The first thing you notice is that the button stroke differs: between DS and DS Lite the stroke is nearly double" (「DSとDS Liteではボタンのストロークがほぼ倍ある」). "This is largely due to the different shape of the rubber part of the switch that contacts the board. When fully pressed, there is some gap before the switch touches the board, so the difference feels like the GBA SP versus the Micro." "The DS feels like clicking a button, while the DS Lite clearly comes with a feeling of pushing" (「DSはどちらかというとボタンをクリックするような感覚だが、DS Liteははっきりと「押す」という感覚を伴う」). The DS mounts its buttons angled slightly outward; the DS Lite's are flatter. ([Game Watch](https://game.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/20060306/dsl.htm); press; the sentence does not say which is longer, but its context says the DS Lite's is)
- Ars Technica: the DS Lite buttons look smaller but "when you put them next to each other you see they're pretty much the same size" as the DS Fat's, "a little bit on the small side, and bunched rather close". ([Ars p.4, archived](https://web.archive.org/web/2006/http://arstechnica.com/reviews/hardware/dslite.ars/4); press)
- Nintendo Life forum (gcunit, 2024-08-30): DS Lite buttons are "mushier than the joy con and Vita buttons", have "more travel and less noise", and "You really have to try to soft-press in order to not make a noise pressing them"; the original poster found the original DS's buttons "more satisfacting than the VITA". ([Nintendo Life forum](https://www.nintendolife.com/forums/ds/how_mushy_are_the_buttons_of_a_nintendo_ds_lite); community claim)
- facelesstech: "people want the DS lite to have clicky buttons like the DS phat/DSi/GBA sp/3DS"; Nintendo went to membrane buttons on the Lite (the author guesses for cost); his clicky retrofit uses "the same buttons as the DS Phat", after a first batch of domes that were "far too annoying to use". ([facelesstech blog](https://facelesstech.wordpress.com/2022/07/23/ds-lite-clicky-buttons/); community claim)
- Pi Tin: "uses the exact same Alps tactile switches and elastomer membrane design used in the Gameboy Advance SP and DSi". Its schematic specifies 8 x **Alps SKRRAAE010** (D-pad and ABXY) and 4 x **Alps SKQGABE010**. ([Pi Tin README](https://github.com/jackw01/pi-tin); [mintypcb.kicad_sch](https://github.com/jackw01/pi-tin/blob/main/pcb_main_r2/mintypcb.kicad_sch); community claim about the DSi)
- Alps SKRRAAE010: 7.5 x 7.0 x 0.6 mm SMD top-push tact switch, **1.0 N operating force, 0.25 mm travel**, 5,000,000 cycles, "Sharp feeling type". SKQGABE010: 5.2 x 5.2 x 1.5 mm, **1.57 N, 0.25 mm**, 1,000,000 cycles. ([Alps SKRRAAE010](https://tech.alpsalpine.com/e/products/detail/SKRRAAE010/), [Alps SKQGABE010](https://tech.alpsalpine.com/e/products/detail/SKQGABE010/); official)
- GBA SP replacement switches: "High Quality Alps Alpine replacement tactile switches ... For D-Pad, A&B, Start/Select, and Brightness. **100gf** Actuator Force"; 6 x 7.5 x 7 x 0.6 mm and 3 x 4.8 x 4.8 x 0.55 mm. The 7.5 x 7 x 0.6 size matches SKRR. ([HHL GBA SP switches](https://handheldlegend.com/products/replacement-tactile-switches-for-game-boy-advance-sp); community claim, vendor)
- The GBA SP "switched from rubber membrane buttons, common on almost all Nintendo systems to this point, to dome tact switches." ([Game Boy Resource](https://gameboy.github.io/consoles/advancesp); community claim)
- Cap underside: Pi Tin's DS-Lite-only part `p013 ds lite abxy button plug x4` is a **5.20 mm diameter disc** (about 1.2 to 1.4 mm thick) with a 1.50 mm pin, 2.15 mm overall, described as "four separate cylindrical plugs that fit into the A/B/X/Y buttons"; the render shows the disc inside each cap's underside. ([Pi Tin build guide](https://github.com/jackw01/pi-tin/blob/main/hardware_build.md), [STL zip](https://github.com/jackw01/pi-tin/blob/main/3d/pi_tin_stl.zip); derived)
- Pi Tin's printed A cap `p005a` (a stand-in for the genuine one, in agreement with DSLITE.md): body 6.99 mm, flange 8.73 x 9.93 mm including the tab, flange about 0.5 mm thick, 3.60 mm tall, **flat top** with an edge round: 6.84 mm across at z 3.30, 6.45 at 3.50, 6.01 at 3.59, i.e. about a 0.5 to 0.6 mm radius. ([STL zip](https://github.com/jackw01/pi-tin/blob/main/3d/pi_tin_stl.zip); derived; replica)
- Legends: on a Polar White DS Lite the caps are white with **pale grey letters showing no visible relief** (they look pad-printed, not moulded), and "START"/"SELECT" are printed on the shell face beside their buttons. ([Commons close-up, 2006](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Nintendo_DS_Lite_(closeup_buttons).jpg), [Commons Sel-Start](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sel-Start.JPG); estimated, photo observation)
- Cap diameter cross-check from the Sel-Start photo, scaled by the 15.0 mm ABXY pitch: A cap 112 px across at 15.7 px/mm, **7.1 mm**, agreeing with DSLITE.md's 7.0. ([Commons Sel-Start](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sel-Start.JPG); estimated)
- ABXY membrane (aftermarket, HHL photos): one piece, four domes at the corners of a square (adjacent spacing 10.6 mm in the console), and a **raised, hollow diamond-shaped boss at the centre** (hollow seen from below, solid diamond seen from above). facelesstech's ABXY flex board has a centre hole that "aligns with the hall effect sensor". ([HHL membranes](https://handheldlegend.com/products/nintendo-ds-lite-silicone-button-pads); [facelesstech blog](https://facelesstech.wordpress.com/2022/07/23/ds-lite-clicky-buttons/); estimated / community claim)
- Visible dome plunger rings in the HHL photo are about 3.1 to 3.3 mm across (12 to 13 px radius at 7.87 px/mm). ([HHL photo NDL1471](https://handheldlegend.com/products/nintendo-ds-lite-silicone-button-pads); estimated)

### Inferences
- Feel model: on the DS Lite the silicone dome is both spring and switch, so the cap travels through the dome's collapse plus a further squeeze before the pill seats (Game Watch's "gap before contact"); that gives a long, soft, quiet stroke with a weak snap. The DS, DSi and GBA SP add a 0.25 mm, roughly 100 gf metal dome under the rubber, so the stroke ends in a sharp click. Because our board uses genuine-style membranes on bare gold pads, we inherit the DS Lite feel automatically; anything that preloads the cap (a lid pocket too shallow, a flange clamped onto the dome) shortens the stroke and stiffens it.
- Cap recess about 5.2 mm across in a 7.0 mm body leaves about a 0.9 mm wall; the silicone plunger (about 3.2 mm) sits inside that recess, so the cap is guided partly by the plunger, not only by the lid hole.
- The ABXY pad's hollow central diamond exists to clear the DS Lite's lid (hall) sensor. On our board it is just a raised feature on the pad's top face at the cluster centre: the lid needs clearance (or a locating pocket) for it between the four caps.
- The "about double" stroke is a felt comparison. If the DS's total key travel is near a tact switch's 0.25 mm plus cap free play, the DS Lite figure is on the order of 0.5 to 1 mm; this is a guess and should not be used as a number.

### Gaps
- Genuine top curvature (flat, slightly domed or slightly dished) of ABXY: no scan, drawing or reliable description. The photos suggest flat with a rounded edge but cannot resolve tenths of a millimetre.
- How legends differ by colour variant or region (for example black or Crimson units): no source found.
- Any force or travel measurement of a genuine DS Lite face button: none found.
- Whether the DSi's face buttons really use Alps SKRR-class tact switches rests on Pi Tin's statement alone.

## 3. Start/Select, and where the LEDs actually are

### Takeaway
Start and Select are **two small round hollow caps stacked vertically, START above SELECT, below and to the left of the ABXY diamond**, on a separate two-dome silicone strip; reviewers found them small and stiff. The brief's assumption that the power and charge LEDs sit near them is wrong for the DS Lite: **the LEDs are on the right end of the hinge**.

### Cited Findings
- Nintendo's manual lists the Control Buttons as "The +Control Pad, A, B, X, and Y Buttons, L and R Buttons, and START and SELECT", and its component drawing shows the two small round buttons below and left of ABXY. ([DS Lite Instruction Booklet, archived](https://web.archive.org/web/2015id_/http://www.nintendo.com/consumer/downloads/DSLite_english.pdf), linked from [Nintendo support](https://en-americas-support.nintendo.com/app/answers/detail/a_id/16905); official)
- Ars: "The start and select buttons have been moved to the bottom of the system and to the right of the screen. They're both round, which can make it more difficult to figure out which button is which without looking." ([Ars p.4, archived](https://web.archive.org/web/2006/http://arstechnica.com/reviews/hardware/dslite.ars/4); press)
- Game Watch: START and SELECT moved and were made smaller, so they are "a little hard for adults to press" (「大人にはちょっと押しにくい」). ([Game Watch](https://game.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/20060306/dsl.htm); press)
- NWR: "The movement of the Select and Start buttons is a welcome change, although they feel very stiff." ([NWR](https://nintendoworldreport.com/hands-on-preview/11190); press)
- Layout from the Sel-Start photo, scaled by the 15.0 mm ABXY pitch separately in each axis to absorb perspective: visible cap (including the hole's edge) about **3.8 mm** across; START to SELECT **about 8.2 mm** centre to centre, vertical; START's centre about **9 mm left of and 25 mm below the ABXY centre**, SELECT's about 34 mm below. ([Commons Sel-Start](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sel-Start.JPG); estimated, plus or minus 10 percent)
- Pi Tin printed Start/Select cap `p008`: body **3.40 mm**, flange **5.00 mm** and about 0.5 mm thick, **2.90 mm tall**, flat top with about a 0.2 mm edge round. ([STL zip](https://github.com/jackw01/pi-tin/blob/main/3d/pi_tin_stl.zip); derived; replica)
- The genuine caps are hollow: "It is necessary to fill the hollow inside of the DS Lite start/select buttons" (STL provided, or hot glue, epoxy or silicone). Pi Tin's filler plug `p015` is **2.20 mm diameter x 1.40 mm**. ([Pi Tin build guide](https://github.com/jackw01/pi-tin/blob/main/hardware_build.md), [STL zip](https://github.com/jackw01/pi-tin/blob/main/3d/pi_tin_stl.zip); community claim / derived)
- Start/Select membrane (aftermarket): one rectangular strip with two round plungers, each standing in a square well; about **15.8 x 8.0 mm** from the photo. ([HHL membranes](https://handheldlegend.com/products/nintendo-ds-lite-silicone-button-pads); estimated)
- LEDs: "the power and charge lamps, which were below the lower screen [on the DS], moved to the right of the hinge that joins the two screens, so they are easy to check when folded" (「ダブルスクリーンをつなぐヒンジの右へと移動」). ([Game Watch](https://game.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/20060306/dsl.htm); press) The manual's callouts 3 and 4 point at the right hinge knuckle, and the Commons close-up shows two slots there. ([manual](https://web.archive.org/web/2015id_/http://www.nintendo.com/consumer/downloads/DSLite_english.pdf); official; [Commons close-up](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Nintendo_DS_Lite_(closeup_buttons).jpg))
- LED behaviour: Power Indicator LED is green, "will change to red when battery charge becomes low", "Rapid blinking shows that wireless communication is in progress", "Slow blinking shows that the Nintendo DS is in Sleep Mode"; Recharge Indicator LED "Lights orange while the battery is being recharged and turns off when charging is complete". ([manual p.5](https://web.archive.org/web/2015id_/http://www.nintendo.com/consumer/downloads/DSLite_english.pdf); official)

### Inferences
- Hollow caps over plungers that stand in wells mean the cap's travel is set by the plunger height and the well, not by the lid. Anyone using a membrane without that plunger (Pi Tin) has to fill the cap; we use genuine-style strips, so the cap stays hollow.
- Why Start/Select feel stiff is not documented. Two plausible contributors: the small cap (3.4 to 3.8 mm) gives a fingertip little area, and a cap that sits low in its hole is hard to reach. A case that recesses these caps further (to stop accidental presses) would make both worse.

### Gaps
- Genuine Start/Select cap dimensions, proud height and flange shape: only Pi Tin's replica and a photo estimate.
- Start/Select pad pitch on the PCB and pad geometry: not found (the membrane README covers only ABXY and D-pad).

## 4. L/R shoulder buttons

### Takeaway
Each shoulder button is a **plastic trigger hinged on a loose metal pin held between the two shell halves, biased by a small spring, pressing a right-angle (side-push) SMD tact switch** soldered at the board's back edge; the click comes from that switch's internal metal dome, which is why L/R feel crisp while the face buttons feel soft. The well-known failure is **the switch itself** (oxidised or fatigued dome), less often dirt, a mis-seated trigger or a switch ripped off its pads. No OEM part number or dimensions were found.

### Cited Findings
- iFixit: the triggers are "held in very loosely by small metal pegs and springs. Don't lose track of these pieces"; once out you "can now access the micro-button component". ([iFixit triggers 4757](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Triggers+Replacement/4757), [iFixit shoulder 122138](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Shoulder+Buttons+Replacement/122138); community claim, repair guide)
- GBAtemp: "the shoulder buttons are small micro switches which are surface mounted on the motherboard"; "the shoulder buttons ride on a post which is held in place by both halves of the shell", with a small spring keeping pressure off the switch; first test with the shell closed and the trigger removed, because a mis-seated trigger is common. ([GBAtemp thread 219409](https://gbatemp.net/threads/l-button-not-responding-working-ds-lite.219409/); search excerpt, community claim)
- Retro Revival (2022-04-06 and 04-07): the shoulder switch is a tactile switch whose "dome spring" lost its "springy" nature after earlier cleaning of oxidation; swapping in a dome and a larger push button from a new switch gave a better "snap" but still poor registration (suspected oxidation under the dome); the fix was a new switch. "The tactile switches for the shoulder buttons are very small, and the contact points are largely under the switch"; removal needed hot air with Kapton, and lifted "the outside traces", which "are both ground traces", with the signal on the middle trace. ([part 1](https://www.retrorevival.ca/2022/04/06/nintendo-ds-lite-shoulder-button-repair), [part 2](https://www.retrorevival.ca/2022/04/07/nintendo-ds-lite-shoulder-button-repair-continued); community claim, hands-on)
- Replacement part as sold: a right-angle SMD tact switch in a metal frame with a round cream plunger on its face and three terminals visible on the bottom edge, "A replacement set of push buttons for your Nintendo DS Lite or Nintendo DSi", with the note "Click test the bumpers before install". ([HHL Replacement Bumper Button, 2x](https://handheldlegend.com/products/copy-of-replacement-bumper-button-for-game-boy-advance-sp-2x); community claim, vendor photo) ZedLabz sells a pair listed for DS Lite, DSi, DSi XL and 3DS; "soldering and console disassembly" required. ([ZedLabz](https://www.zedlabz.com/en-us/products/ds-dsi-xl-shoulder-trigger-buttons); community claim)
- Other failures: a trigger switch that "snapped off the board" on a previously opened unit (iFixit comment, 2020-08-27); cleaning the contact fixed it without replacement (iFixit comments 2023-01-28 and 2024-05-25). ([iFixit 4757](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Triggers+Replacement/4757), [iFixit 122138](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Shoulder+Buttons+Replacement/122138); community claim)
- Feel: "the shoulder buttons hug the system much more closely, and have a great feel to them" (Ars); L/R "shapes differ [from the DS], so ease of pressing will depend on finger length" (Game Watch). ([Ars p.4](https://web.archive.org/web/2006/http://arstechnica.com/reviews/hardware/dslite.ars/4), [Game Watch](https://game.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/20060306/dsl.htm); press)
- Analogue, GBA SP (not DS Lite): "the internals of the shoulder buttons tend to accumulate build-up and no longer make electrical contact when actuated". ([Game Boy Resource](https://gameboy.github.io/consoles/advancesp); community claim)

### Inferences
- The shell carries the hinge (pin trapped between halves) and the spring, the board carries only the switch. For our case this means the trigger pin bosses must be split across the tub and lid and must line up with a board-edge switch to within a fraction of a millimetre; misalignment either preloads the switch (false presses) or leaves a dead zone.
- Because the dome is inside a sealed tiny switch, the usual fix is replacement, and the pads are under the body: on our board give the L/R footprint generous pads that extend beyond the body so hand rework does not lift traces (Retro Revival lost two).
- A switch of this kind (side-push SMD tact switch in a metal frame with a round plunger) is a catalogue category; a datasheet part with a similar plunger height should be chosen and fitted to our own trigger rather than chasing the unmarked OEM part. Its size cannot be read from the vendor photo (no scale).

### Gaps
- OEM switch maker, part number, body size, plunger height, force and travel: not found. Seller codes (ZL400399, NDSiBTTNSWCH10P) are not manufacturer part numbers.
- Trigger pin diameter, spring type (coil or torsion) and trigger travel: not stated by any source reached. Measure on a donor shell.

## 5. Power slide switch and volume slider

### Takeaway
The power control is a **spring-return (momentary) SMD slide switch, SW13, on the bottom of the board**, worked by a separate plastic slider in the right side of the shell; you push it **"UP" and hold briefly to turn off**. The volume control is a **slider on the front edge, left end, "Slide to the right to increase volume"**; the electrical part is a metal-can SMD slide component (most likely a slide potentiometer). Both shell sliders are removable with tweezers and both electrical parts break if the shell slider is misaligned at reassembly.

### Cited Findings
- Manual, Power Button (POWER): "Turns system on or off. To prevent turning the system off accidentally during game play, you must hold the Power Button in the UP position for a moment before it will turn off." Volume Control (VOL.): "Slide to the right to increase volume." The drawing places POWER (callout 5) on the right end of the lower half and VOL. (callout 9) on the front edge at the left end, with Slot-2 (10) and the headphone/mic jacks (11) also on the front edge. ([manual p.4 to 5](https://web.archive.org/web/2015id_/http://www.nintendo.com/consumer/downloads/DSLite_english.pdf); official)
- Game Watch: the DS's push-type power button at the lower left became "a slide switch at the right side of the lower screen" that toggles power with each slide, to stop accidental power-offs when lifting the unit; "the volume switch got bigger and is easier to slide" (「ボリュームスイッチは大型化し、スライドさせやすい」). ([Game Watch](https://game.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/20060306/dsl.htm); press)
- repair.wiki: "The power button is located on the bottom side of the PCB labelled SW13. The switch has 4 pins." At rest pin 2 to pin 3 is shorted and pin 1 and pin 4 are open to everything; operated, "pin 1 should now be shorted to pin 2 and 3". ([repair.wiki](https://repair.wiki/w/Nintendo_DS_Lite_not_turning_on), [archived copy](https://web.archive.org/web/2025/https://repair.wiki/w/Nintendo_DS_Lite_not_turning_on); community claim, repair wiki)
- The replacement part sold for it is a metal-shelled SMD slide switch with a short vertical actuator lever. ([HHL DS Lite Power Switch](https://handheldlegend.com/products/nintendo-ds-lite-power-switch-x2); community claim, vendor photo)
- Failure mode: "The tip that moves with the plastic to turn the handheld on/off has broken off" (iFixit comment 2020-09-06); fix is to desolder and replace, keeping the iron off the top "as that may melt the moving parts together" (Emily H, 2021-09-11). Tim Anderson (2011-05-28): the plastic slider "was slightly misaligned" at reassembly and "broke the small protrusion on the internal switch", leaving it operable only with a pin; replaced the surface-mount switch. ([iFixit 1942](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Power+Switch+Replacement/1942), [itwriting.com](https://www.itwriting.com/blog/tag/ds-lite); community claim)
- With the switch's lever broken the shell slider "felt springless", and the switch could be worked with a paper clip. ([GBAtemp 343896](https://gbatemp.net/threads/broken-ds-lite-power-switch.343896/); search excerpt, community claim)
- iFixit (oldturkey03, 2020-12-08): "If it is just the slider, you can replace that. If it is the actual switch, it needs to be replaced and will require soldering." ([iFixit Q&A 669956](https://www.ifixit.com/Answers/View/669956/Ds+lite+power+switch+broken); community claim)
- iFixit guides: "make sure that power switch and volume sliders are properly aligned with the switches on the motherboard"; "Some units are sealed with adhesive, and pulling too hard can damage the power switch"; both plastic sliders lift out with tweezers. ([iFixit 1942](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Nintendo+DS+Lite+Power+Switch+Replacement/1942), [iFixit 1943](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Repairing+Nintendo+DS+Lite+Volume+Switch/1943); community claim)
- HHL's "Volume Switch Button" replacement photo shows a metal-can SMD slide component with its actuator protruding from the long side and gull-wing terminals. ([HHL volume switch](https://handheldlegend.com/products/nintendo-ds-lite-volume-switch-button-replacement); community claim, vendor photo)

### Inferences
- "Hold in the UP position for a moment" plus "springless when the lever breaks" means the return spring is inside SW13, not in the shell slider; the firmware times the hold. In the manual's drawing the switch is on the right end face, so "UP" points toward the hinge (derived from the drawing's orientation).
- SW13's always-shorted pin pair (2 and 3) and isolated pin 4 suggest a double-throw part used single-throw, with pin 4 as the unused throw or a mechanical tab.
- The shell slider is a separate moulding whose tang must land inside the switch lever's travel window; most "broken switch" reports are this tang shearing the lever at reassembly. A case that uses a real slide switch should key the slider so it cannot be assembled out of the lever.

### Gaps
- Power and volume travel in millimetres, operating force, and part numbers: none found.
- Whether the volume part is a potentiometer or a multi-position switch is not stated by any source reached; gorgyrip's USG-CPU-10 schematic (BitBuilt Dropbox, 1.35 GB) would settle it.

## 6. Quantitative feel data: forces and travel, and the nearest analogues

### Takeaway
**No measured actuation force or travel exists in public for any DS Lite control** (button, membrane, trigger or slider). The usable numbers are analogues: the GBA SP's Alps tact switches at **100 gf / 1.0 N with 0.25 mm travel**, and general silicone-keypad design rules (**80 to 150 g actuation, 40 to 60 percent snap ratio, about 0.8 mm stroke, 30 to 35 g return**). Qualitatively, the DS Lite's face-button stroke is about twice the original DS's.

### Cited Findings
- No grams-force or newton value for DS Lite buttons turned up in English or Japanese searches; the only DS Lite "force" figure found (1.5 to 2.0 N for a power switch) is on an AliExpress page that says it may be AI-generated. ([AliExpress Wiki](https://www.aliexpress.com/s/wiki-ssr/article/replacement-power-switch-button); unreliable, excluded)
- Stroke ratio: DS Lite about double the DS (Game Watch). ([Game Watch](https://game.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/20060306/dsl.htm); press)
- GBA SP face and D-pad switches: 100 gf, 7.5 x 7 x 0.6 mm and 4.8 x 4.8 x 0.55 mm Alps parts. ([HHL](https://handheldlegend.com/products/replacement-tactile-switches-for-game-boy-advance-sp); community claim) Alps SKRRAAE010: 1.0 N, 0.25 mm; SKQGABE010: 1.57 N, 0.25 mm. ([Alps](https://tech.alpsalpine.com/e/products/detail/SKRRAAE010/), [Alps](https://tech.alpsalpine.com/e/products/detail/SKQGABE010/); official)
- Diamond HMI Rubber Keypad Design Guide (2025): snap ratio = (F1 minus F2) / F1 with F1 actuation and F2 contact force, target 40 to 60 percent; an actuation force of 125 to 150 g suits most uses; 80 to 150 g for keys 10 to 15 mm high and 150 to 175 g for keys 15 to 25 mm high; "Keep stroke as near 0.8 mm as possible"; return force should be about 30 to 35 g "to ensure that keys do not stick"; to reduce rocking add stabilising posts or multiple contacts per key, keep the web short and its angle near 40 degrees; silicone hardness 40 to 80 Shore A, typically 65 plus or minus 5. ([Diamond HMI guide PDF](https://www.diamondhmi.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Rubber-keypad-design-guide-2025.pdf); official, manufacturer guide)
- Same guide, carbon pills: "long life (>30 million ops) low resistance (<150 Ω with 100g load)", diameters 2.0 to 8.0 mm and 0.3 to 0.8 mm thick; its table gives about 30 to 60 ohms for 3.5 to 4.0 mm pills depending on thickness and texture; pads should be at least 1.25 times the pill. ([Diamond HMI guide PDF](https://www.diamondhmi.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Rubber-keypad-design-guide-2025.pdf); official)
- Epectec design guide: typical silicone key travel 0.8 to 1.5 mm, forces 60 to 300 g. ([Epectec](https://www.epectec.com/keypads/design); search excerpt, official)

### Inferences
- A DS Lite face button is probably in the generic 80 to 150 g band with a weak snap, and its travel is several times a tact switch's 0.25 mm; but every one of those words is analogy. The only way to get real numbers for our build is to measure: a kitchen scale under a cap pressed by a probe, reading the peak (F1) and the dip at contact (F2), on genuine and on HHL pads.
- Because force falls with wear and aftermarket silicone varies (section 8), any number measured should be taken on several pads.

### Gaps
- Force curves for genuine DS Lite domes, Start/Select, L/R and the power switch: none found in teardowns, part listings, hobbyist force-curve sites or video descriptions searched.

## 7. Membrane outline and rest height (DSLITE.md "Still unknown")

### Takeaway
The **outlines can now be estimated** (all from an aftermarket product photo scaled by the 15.0 mm ABXY pitch, plus or minus 1 mm): ABXY pad about **27 mm tip to tip** along the button axes, D-pad pad about **24 mm**, Start/Select strip about **15.8 x 8.0 mm**. The **rest height (`dome_h`) is still unknown**; nothing public gives it.

### Cited Findings
- ABXY pad (photo NDL1471, 7.87 px/mm from the 10.6 mm adjacent dome spacing): four-lobed outline; lobe tips **13.5 mm** from the cluster centre along the dome axes (27.1 mm across, i.e. through Y and A); the bounding box in the 45-degree directions is **22.9 x 22.8 mm**; the notches between lobes are **9.2 mm** from the centre; each lobe is roughly a **6.0 mm radius** around its dome. ([HHL membranes](https://handheldlegend.com/products/nintendo-ds-lite-silicone-button-pads); estimated)
- D-pad pad (same photo, assuming a 14.0 mm pitch, 8.13 px/mm): lobe tips about **12.1 mm** from centre (about 24 mm across), 45-degree bounding box about **20.2 mm**, notches about 7.9 mm from centre, lobes about 5.0 mm radius; central hole about 3 mm. ([HHL membranes](https://handheldlegend.com/products/nintendo-ds-lite-silicone-button-pads); estimated)
- Start/Select strip: about **15.8 x 8.0 mm**, two plungers in square wells. ([HHL membranes](https://handheldlegend.com/products/nintendo-ds-lite-silicone-button-pads); estimated)
- The pads have molded channels on the underside running from the domes toward the centre and the edges (visible through the clear and white variants). ([HHL photo H9fd9cbd0](https://handheldlegend.com/products/nintendo-ds-lite-silicone-button-pads); estimated, photo observation)
- Pointers already in DSLITE.md (face plate 1.4 to 1.5 mm, board top 3.0 to 3.4 mm under the face, from an aftermarket shell scan) were not re-checked here.

### Inferences
- In the console the ABXY pad sits with its lobes on the A, B, X and Y axes, so its widest extent (about 27 mm) runs horizontally and vertically, and the narrowest gaps between lobes (about 18 mm across the notches) fall on the diagonals. `piece_d` in case.scad should be about 27 mm if it models a circle that clears the lobes, or the lid can follow the four-lobe outline more tightly.
- The membrane lobe (about 6.0 mm radius) extends about 1.6 mm beyond the ABXY flange radius (4.4 mm), so the lid's flange pocket does not by itself clamp the silicone; the web outside the flange needs its own clearance or a light clamp ring.
- Rest height must be measured. The cheapest route: press a genuine pad between two flat plates with a 0.05 mm feeler stack, or section one pad with a blade and photograph it against a rule.

### Gaps
- Dome height at rest (board to the top of a plunger), web thickness, and dome collapse distance: not published anywhere found. Genuine-pad outlines may differ from HHL's aftermarket copies.

## 8. Genuine versus aftermarket membranes and caps

### Takeaway
Aftermarket DS Lite membranes (Shenzhen-made, sold by HHL and others in many colours) mostly work, but every failure report has the same shape: **presses that need extra force or register only at some angles, and diagonals that drop out**, usually cured by going back to genuine or fresher pads. There is **no published way to tell genuine from aftermarket**, and no reputable vendor sells certified-genuine new DS Lite membranes.

### Cited Findings
- HHL's "Nintendo DS Lite Silicone Membranes" are third-party ("independent, third-party restoration or accessory product"), made by Shenzhen Speed Sources Technology, SKU NDSL3000, USD 3.49, in Gray, Blue, Clear, Purple, Red, Yellow, Green, Black and Pink; one D-pad pad, one ABXY pad, one Start/Select strip. Of 18 reviews, 17 are five-star ("as good as the original factory parts"); the one-star review says buttons needed hard presses and sometimes failed, and refitting the original membranes fixed it. ([HHL](https://handheldlegend.com/products/nintendo-ds-lite-silicone-button-pads); community claim)
- A refurbished DS Lite whose face buttons ignored presses depending on the angle and whose D-pad felt loose next to an original; a reply said aftermarket parts show this, "some better than others", and suspected the caps rather than the membrane. ([GBAtemp 624369](https://gbatemp.net/threads/replacing-buttons-from-refurbished-ds-lite.624369/); search excerpt, community claim)
- A replacement membrane that was itself worn out (diagonals) until a third one was fitted. ([iFixit Q&A 930769](https://www.ifixit.com/Answers/View/930769/D-pad+diagonal+inputs+don't+register+correctly); community claim)
- Aftermarket caps exist in matte "Soft touch" and chrome finishes; eXtremeRate's DS Lite set includes A, B, X, Y, D-pad, Start, Select, Power, Volume, L and R. ([HHL eXtremeRate DS Lite buttons](https://handheldlegend.com/products/button-set-for-nintendo-ds-lite-extremerate); community claim, vendor)
- Pi Tin: if its membranes are printed in rigid plastic instead of 95A TPU, "the buttons will be louder and clickier", and its D-pad nub must be trimmed. ([Pi Tin build guide](https://github.com/jackw01/pi-tin/blob/main/hardware_build.md); community claim)

### Inferences
- Angle-dependent registration points to cap or pad height mismatch (a cap that rocks before the pill seats flat) more than to pill conductivity; it is exactly what a small extra dome height or a loose cap in its hole produces. For our build, test each membrane lot on the coupon with genuine caps before committing, and prefer the pad lot that registers at the lowest press with the cap pressed off-centre.
- Colour is no guide to genuineness, since aftermarket pads come in grey and clear too.

### Gaps
- How to identify a genuine Nintendo DS Lite membrane (moulding marks, colour, pill finish): no source found.
- Any side-by-side measurement of genuine versus aftermarket hardness or height: none found.
- Reputable sources of genuine pads: only used pulls from donor consoles (eBay lots of "original" parts exist but could not be inspected; pages blocked).

## 9. Clearances, retention and guidance for printed shells that take genuine caps

### Takeaway
The genuine shell gives an ABXY cap about **0.6 mm diametral clearance** (7.6 mm hole on a 7.0 mm cap) and retains it purely by the **flange under the face plate**, keyed by a tab; Pi Tin, a working printed shell for genuine caps, runs tighter at **0.34 mm** but still tells builders the buttons "should fit loosely" and to trim the ABXY flanges if needed. Too tight and the weak silicone return (about 30 to 35 g in generic designs) cannot beat friction, so caps stick; too loose and caps rock and register only at some angles.

### Cited Findings
- Hole sizes already in DSLITE.md: DS Lite face 7.6 mm (ABXY) and 18.9 mm across the D-pad cross; Pi Tin 7.34 mm and an 18.2 x 6.2 mm cross opening for a 7.0 mm cap and a 17.8 x 5.9 mm cross. ([DSLITE.md](../../../../../hardware/ref_design/case/DSLITE.md); [Pi Tin](https://github.com/jackw01/pi-tin))
- Pi Tin assembly: "Insert the buttons into the back of the front panel as shown. The A/B/X/Y buttons can only be inserted one way. The buttons should fit loosely; if using DS Lite A/B/X/Y buttons, the flanges of the buttons may need to be carefully trimmed with a craft knife or flush cutters." Membranes printed in 95A TPU at 0.1 mm layers; rigid membranes make the buttons louder and clickier. ([Pi Tin build guide](https://github.com/jackw01/pi-tin/blob/main/hardware_build.md); community claim)
- Diamond HMI clearance rules for rubber keys through a plastic case: case opening minus key size at least **0.4 mm** in one axis and **0.3 mm** in the other; key height above the plastic minus stroke at least **1.0 mm**; stop-bar stroke minus key stroke at least **0.15 mm**; minimum **0.2 mm** radius on key side and top edges; return force **30 to 35 g** so keys do not stick; minimum key height = base thickness + bezel thickness + stroke + 0.5 mm. ([Diamond HMI guide PDF](https://www.diamondhmi.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Rubber-keypad-design-guide-2025.pdf); official, generic rubber keys, not DS Lite)
- Sticking in the field is mostly debris: a sticking DS Lite R button fixed "for a while" by compressed air underneath. ([Penny Arcade forum](https://forums.penny-arcade.com/discussion/79421/my-ds-has-a-semi-broken-button); search excerpt, community claim)
- An iFixit question about a DS Lite after a shell swap: "All the buttons worked fine before but now most of the buttons are responsive!" (the excerpt reads as written; the title and context suggest the buttons stopped working; no fix is given). ([iFixit answer history 20944](https://nl.ifixit.com/Answers/History/20944); search excerpt, community claim, ambiguous)

### Inferences
- Radial clearance: genuine about 0.3 mm per side, Pi Tin about 0.17 mm per side. A printed lid should start near the genuine 7.6 mm and only tighten after a fit test, because FDM holes print undersize and the cap's flange tab must also slide freely in its slot. The DSLITE.md value of 7.4 mm sits between the two and is reasonable for a resin print; for FDM, 7.5 to 7.6 mm is safer.
- Retention is all flange: the pocket must be deeper than the flange (0.5 to 0.6 mm) by enough that the flange never touches the lid at rest, otherwise the lid preloads the dome, steals stroke and stiffens the press. Pi Tin's flange trimming advice suggests its pocket is near that limit.
- Reshell failures (buttons dead after a new shell) are what happens when the face-to-board distance of the new shell is even a few tenths of a millimetre larger than genuine: the caps hang below the domes' contact point. Our lid-to-board stack therefore needs the measured `dome_h` before final dimensions, as DSLITE.md already says; the button coupons are the right test.
- The D-pad needs its own rule: its centre peg, not the hole, should set its height, so the cross opening can stay loose (genuine 18.9 mm across on a 17.8 mm cross, about 0.55 mm per arm end) without the D-pad rattling.

### Gaps
- A modder's measured hole clearance for genuine DS Lite caps in an FDM print (as opposed to Pi Tin's design value) was not found.
- Other open shells built around genuine DS Lite controls (Game Boy Macro shells, MacroSOS on Printables) could not be read (Printables blocked automated fetches); they would give a second face-to-board distance.
